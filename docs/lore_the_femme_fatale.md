# The Femme Fatale — Princes' Operative

## Brainstorm Session: March 14, 2026

*Status: Raw character concept. Nothing final.*

---

## Who She Is

A Princes' elite operative. Not a soldier — an *instrument*. The Princes don't send her when they want a fight. They send her when they want a *result*. Assassination, data theft, evidence planting, sabotage. She works alone, she's terrifyingly competent, and she has her own style.

Think Leon (the movie) — that feeling of someone who knows exactly what they're doing, who has prepared meticulously, who treats killing as craft.

---

## Visual / Vibe

- Tight tactical clothing, athletic build
- Carries a massive anti-materiel sniper rifle — something that looks like it belongs on a vehicle mount. 40-100kg, stationary, bipod, stabilizers, a scope that costs more than a house
- Patient. Professional. One shot. Then disassemble, pack, and ghost
- Cloaking capability — she can move through ships near-invisible

---

## Role in the Story

She's a recurring character — not a one-off. The player encounters her shadow throughout the game:

- **First appearance**: the assassination (see mission concept below). The player sees the aftermath or witnesses the shot. She's gone before anyone can respond.
- **Mid-game encounters**: the squad boards ships and finds security systems already bypassed — she got there first. Spotted briefly on camera feeds: cloaked figure, moving fast, carrying something out of a server room. By the time the squad arrives, she's gone.
- **She does spec ops alongside the player**: runs into ships while the player is clearing them, cloaks, steals or plants things, does her own mission in parallel. Sometimes her objectives conflict with the player's. Sometimes they accidentally align.
- **Late-game**: the player finally encounters her directly. Does she fight? Talk? Maybe she has information the player needs. Maybe she's questioning her employers. Maybe she's just amused that someone finally caught up.

She's the Princes' version of what the grays are to the cosmic layer — a shadow that moves through the same spaces as the player, always one step ahead, with different objectives on the same board.

## The Assassination — Mission Concept

### The Scene

A popular political figure — someone the people love. A reformer threatening the CBD currency system, or a whistleblower about to go public. The Princes need them gone.

She's set up on a rooftop or a ship's observation deck. Prone behind the massive rifle. She's been here for hours. Patient. The scope tracks the target through a crowd, through security, through a speech. One shot. Clean. Then she disassembles, packs, and vanishes.

### How the Player Fits (Option C — preferred)

The squad is the protection detail. Assigned to protect this political figure. The mission is tense but seems routine — crowd control, sweep the perimeter, watch the rooftops. Then the shot comes. The VIP is dead. The squad failed.

The political consequences hit immediately: your squad was responsible, and now a beloved leader is dead on your watch. This feeds directly into the frame-up plot — the assassination was *designed* to happen on your watch, so your squad takes the blame. The Princes killed their target AND discredited the player's unit in one move.

### Post-Assassination

- The player pursues the shooter's position — finds the rifle mount still warm, the position meticulously cleaned. She left nothing.
- The news cycle turns on the squad. Media manipulation kicks in (the Chase Hughes systems). The squad becomes the story, not the real assassin.
- This event could be the trigger that puts the player on the defensive — now they're not just investigating, they're being investigated. The frame-up is in motion.

## The Honeypot — Mission/Story Beat Concept

_Added: 2026-03-22 (from brainstorm session)_

Before the assassination mission, or in between missions, the player encounters an
attractive NPC — charming, interested, seemingly trustworthy. This is her in a different
role. Not the sniper — the intelligence operative. The seduction happens outside the
tactical layer: a station hub, a bar, a comms channel.

**Mechanical consequences** (not just cutscene drama):
- Mission parameters leaked — enemies know you're coming, positions adjusted
- Squad composition exposed — counters deployed against your loadout
- A safehouse compromised — lose access to a resupply point or intel cache

**Player culpability**: There should be warning signs the player can choose to ignore.
The sting hits harder when the player made a choice. Were there inconsistencies in
her story? Did she ask too-specific questions about the next operation?

**The reveal**: Multiple options —
- Mid-mission: she trips an alarm from inside, or enemies are waiting exactly where
  she'd know to place them
- Post-debrief: something doesn't add up in the timeline
- Much later: the damage compounds before the player connects the dots

**Recurring threat**: She escapes, creating a nemesis with personal history. Someone
who knows your squad, your habits, your weaknesses. This feeds the Princes' information
advantage throughout the campaign.

**Chase Hughes connection**: This character should be written using the influence
techniques from `narrative_media_systems_update_2026-03-08.md` — pacing and leading
in dialogue, embedded commands, utilisation of player choices. She should feel
compelling in ways the player can't quite articulate, which makes the betrayal hit
harder. See also: trance induction techniques (note in narrative doc).
