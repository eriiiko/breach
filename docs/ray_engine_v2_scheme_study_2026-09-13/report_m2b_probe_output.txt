TEST C — WHAT THE SCHEME'S OWN THEORY SAYS AT LOW CAPACITY
==============================================================================
  ours:      f = 1 / max(1, g),          g = 4L/T_abs
  classical: alpha = 1/(1 + beta*c*sigma_a*dt), beta = 4aT^3/C_v
  The IMC 'Fleck number' is exactly our g: beta*c*sigma_a*dt is the
  ratio of the radiative emission RATE to the material's own heat
  content per unit temperature, over one timestep. Check numerically:

         row      T   g = 4L/T_abs    4*a*sigma*A*T^3*dt/C    ratio
        wood   1200        0.02427                 0.02418   1.0039
        wood   5000        1.07899                 1.07736   1.0015
        door   1200        0.00038                 0.00038   1.0039
        door   5000        0.01686                 0.01683   1.0015
   furniture   1200        0.00674                 0.00672   1.0039
   furniture   5000        0.29972                 0.29927   1.0015

  => our g IS the classical Fleck number for this cell, to the
     E-table's own bucket resolution (the small excess subtracted).
  The two forms then differ ONLY in the damping shape:

           g   ours 1/max(1,g)   IMC 1/(1+g)   Fleck floor a=1/2
        0.01            1.0000        0.9901              0.9950
        0.10            1.0000        0.9091              0.9524
        0.50            1.0000        0.6667              0.8000
        1.00            1.0000        0.5000              0.6667
        2.00            0.5000        0.3333              0.5000
       10.00            0.1000        0.0909              0.1667
       77.00            0.0130        0.0128              0.0253

  Ours is the LEAST damping of the three and agrees with them to
  O(g) as g -> 0, so at the live scale (g <= 0.03 everywhere in the
  fire range) all three forms are 'no damping' to within 3 %.

  THE STABILITY-LIMITED EFFECTIVE RATE at low capacity: with f = 1/g
  the per-tick drop is L*f = T_abs/4 exactly, independent of L. That
  is the scheme's ceiling, and it is a TEMPERATURE cap, not an energy
  cap: a lighter cell hits it at a lower temperature but sheds less
  energy doing so. Numerically, for a = 0.9:
