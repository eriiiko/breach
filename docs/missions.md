## Mission 1: Silent Cargo (Opening Mission)

### The Hook

A WHO medical transport — the *MV Caduceus* — entered national waters three days ago carrying humanitarian vaccine supplies. Routine docking was expected. Instead: silence. No radio contact. No crew movement visible on satellite. The ship is drifting.

This is politically sensitive. WHO ships are sovereign — boarding one without permission is an international incident. But people are nervous. The government can't be seen ignoring a ghost ship off the coast. Your squad is sent quietly: investigate, report, get out. No media. No footprint.

**Player briefing**: "Board the vessel. Determine crew status. Secure the bridge. Report findings. Rules of engagement: defensive only — these are medical personnel."

### What the Player Finds

The squad breaches through a lower cargo bay. The ship is dark — emergency lighting only. Power is on but systems are in standby. The air smells wrong.

**Phase 1: Cargo Hold** — crates everywhere, WHO markings, medical supplies. Some crates are smashed open. Spilled vials, broken cold-storage units. A body — but it's not dead. It moves wrong. The first zombie encounter. The player's rules of engagement said "defensive only" — but these things aren't crew anymore. The ROE becomes irrelevant fast.

The opening combat is tight, claustrophobic. Cargo containers create natural cover and chokepoints. The player learns movement, shooting, cover mechanics against a slow, low-threat enemy. Zombies teach the basics without being overwhelming.

**Phase 2: Crew Quarters & Medical Bay** — the squad pushes deeper into the ship. More zombies, some in medical scrubs, some in crew uniforms. The medical bay is trashed — someone tried to barricade themselves in. It didn't work. Medical logs on a terminal (if the player checks): symptoms started 36 hours after the crew handled certain crates. Fever, confusion, aggression. The ship's doctor logged three cases before the entries stop mid-sentence.

Environmental storytelling: a half-eaten meal in the mess hall. Personal effects scattered in corridors. This was fast — the crew didn't have time to organize. The player pieces together a collapse that took maybe 48 hours.

**Phase 3: The Bridge** — the squad fights up through stairwells and corridors to the command bridge. The bridge door is sealed from inside. When the squad breaches it:

The bridge is empty. No zombies. No bodies. But it's *wrong*:
- The navigation console has been wiped — not destroyed, *wiped*. Someone methodically deleted the ship's course logs, communication records, and cargo manifest.
- A cold-storage medical case is open on the captain's chair. Whatever was in it is gone. The case has no WHO markings — it's unmarked, featureless, made of a material the squad doesn't recognize.
- The bridge windows show a clean line of sight to the helipad on the upper deck. A faint scorch mark on the helipad surface — not from a helicopter. The heat pattern is wrong. Something landed and left, but it wasn't any aircraft in the database.

**Phase 4: The Kill Team** — as the squad secures the bridge and starts downloading whatever fragments of data remain, the situation changes. A heavily armed tactical team breaches the ship from multiple entry points. These aren't zombies — they're professional operators. Fast, coordinated, using flashbangs and suppressive fire.

They're not here to rescue anyone. They're here to destroy evidence — and that includes the player's squad. They've brought demolition charges. The ship is going to the bottom of the ocean, and anyone who saw what's on board goes with it.

The player now has to fight through the kill team *and* the remaining zombies to reach an exit point. The kill team treats zombies as hostiles too — three-way chaos in the corridors. The squad can use this: lure zombies into kill team positions, let them thin each other out.

The escape is frantic. The player reaches the extraction point (the cargo bay they entered through, or a lifeboat station) as demolition charges start detonating. The ship is sinking. The squad barely makes it out.

**Who sent the kill team?** The player doesn't know. The debrief doesn't explain it. Their superiors act like it didn't happen. This is the first real sign that the conspiracy extends into the player's own chain of command.

**What the player takes away**: zombies are real, the WHO ship was carrying something dangerous, someone else was here before them (the wiped console, the scorch mark), and now someone tried to kill them to bury the whole thing. The mission ends with more questions than answers — and a strong sense that the player's own side isn't safe. The scorch mark and the strange case are details the player might forget — until they see something similar ten missions later.

### What Actually Happened (Hidden from the Player)

The *Caduceus* was carrying standard WHO medical supplies — plus, unknowingly, a batch of gray anti-xenomorph bioagent disguised as routine vaccines. Crates broke during rough seas. Crew members handling the spill were exposed. Within 48 hours, the mutation spread through the crew.

The grays monitored the situation remotely. When it became clear the ship was lost, they boarded (the scorch mark), retrieved the remaining bioagent samples from the bridge (the unmarked case), wiped all records that could trace the cargo's origin, and left. They were gone hours before the player's squad arrived. The grays don't care about the crew. They care about evidence.

### Design Notes

- **Tutorial function**: Phase 1-3 teaches movement, cover, shooting against slow enemies (zombies). Phase 4 escalates to fast, coordinated human enemies — the player learns that tactics matter against real opponents. Two difficulty curves in one mission
- **Tone setter**: establishes the game's atmosphere — dark, tense, something is very wrong and nobody is telling you the full story
- **Mystery seed**: the wiped console, the strange case, the scorch mark — these are Chekhov's guns for the gray storyline. Planted early, paid off late
- **No gray combat**: the player doesn't see or fight grays here. They only see traces. This is important — the grays are introduced as *absence*, not presence
- **Political layer**: the post-mission debrief is tense. WHO denies the ship was carrying anything unusual. Your superiors want the incident buried. The player's report gets redacted. First taste of the cover-up machinery

## Mission Ideas (Future — Unwritten)

*Rough concepts to develop. Each should introduce or deepen a creature type, a lore thread, or a gameplay mechanic.*

- **Organ harvesting ship** — board a Princes-owned luxury vessel. Upper decks are lavish (the "establishment" rooms). Lower decks hide the horror. Introduces the Princes as antagonists. Human enemies (guards, security) plus creature containment that can go wrong.
- **Xenomorph containment breach** — a research station has lost containment. Military-grade threat. The player might find evidence that the containment was sabotaged — by the grays? To prevent humans from studying the specimens?
- **Political extraction gone wrong** — extract a VIP from a ship. Simple mission, except the VIP knows something. Mid-mission, things escalate. Introduces intelligent tactical enemies (genetic soldiers).
- **The audit** — break into a central bank data facility on a ship. Pure infiltration, stealth-focused. Connects to the Princes/CBD currency storyline.
- **First gray encounter** — mid-to-late game. The player boards a ship and finds a gray lab. The grays are still there. First psychic attack. The squad barely survives. Everything the player thought they knew shifts.
- **Return to the Caduceus** — late game. The player returns to the WHO ship from Mission 1, now with more knowledge. New areas accessible. Finds something in the lower hold they missed the first time — or something that wasn't there before.
