# Predator–Prey, Ecosystem, Artificial-Life & Animal-Behavior RL — Digest for Breach

*Raw research-agent output, 2026-07-20. Exploratory theme: wild AND tame animals, RL-trained; predators+prey that train together (co-adaptation); animals that learn to react to dangers; taming/domestication.*

**Orientation.** Breach gives three things this literature had to build from scratch: (1) determinism (reproducible self-play, debuggable emergent behavior), (2) GPU-residency + massive parallelism (population-scale/autocurriculum methods become affordable), (3) stacked [C,H,W] grid observations (convolutional policies + count/novelty bonuses over cells are natural). Dominant lesson: **you rarely hand-design animal behavior — you design a pressure (a predator, a scarcity, a competitor) and let an autocurriculum or evolutionary loop discover the behavior.** Breach's thesis exactly.

## 1. Competitive co-evolution / emergent complexity
- **Emergent Complexity via Multi-Agent Competition** — Bansal et al. (OpenAI) 2017, arXiv:1710.03748. Competitive 3D self-play (sumo, kick-and-defend) → skills far more complex than the environment; the opponent population is a natural curriculum. Introduces **opponent sampling** (train vs older frozen versions to avoid collapse) + exploration-reward annealing (dense early, sparse late). *Breach:* canonical recipe for co-training predator vs prey; keep a versioned zoo of frozen opponents cheaply.
- **Emergent Tool Use from Multi-Agent Autocurricula (hide-and-seek)** — Baker et al. (OpenAI) 2019, arXiv:1909.07528. Pure hide-and-seek → six escalating strategy regimes (fort-building, ramp use, ramp-locking, box-surfing). Each side's adaptation creates the next problem — self-supervised autocurriculum. Code: github.com/openai/multi-agent-emergence-environments. *Breach:* the north star; Breach's grid + movable-object physics is close to the hide-and-seek substrate.
- **Autocurricula ... A Manifesto** — Leibo et al. (DeepMind) 2019, arXiv:1903.00742. Framework naming the phenomenon; externally-imposed vs emergent (auto-) curricula. *Breach:* vocabulary for why you don't hand-script difficulty ramps.
- **AlphaStar league** — Vinyals et al. 2019, Nature. Main agent + main exploiters + league exploiters + fictitious self-play. *Breach:* if two-species self-play cycles (prey beats predator beats prey…), exploiter/league is the proven stabilizer.

## 2. Predator–prey pursuit–evasion RL
- **MADDPG** — Lowe et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.02275. MPE "simple_tag" predator-prey; slower predators learn to cooperatively corner faster prey. *Breach:* standard baseline + toy task; grid predator-prey = "simple_tag" with real physics.
- **Aquarium** — Kölle et al. 2024, arXiv:2401.07056 (ICAART). Open-source PettingZoo predator-prey; PPO prey evasion; **parameter sharing** across same-species sharply improves coordination. *Breach:* most directly reusable design template; share weights within a species from day one.
- **MAgent** — Zheng et al. 2018 (AAAI), arXiv:1712.00600. ~1M agents on one GPU; Pursuit → encircling closures; Battle → resource-competition dilemmas. *Breach:* proof grid-based GPU high-population predator-prey is tractable.
- **OPEN (Multi-UAV Pursuit-Evasion)** — 2024, arXiv:2409.15866. MARL + evader-prediction network for partial observability + adaptive environment generator. *Breach:* handle prey hidden behind walls via a learned predictor head.
- **MatrixWorld** — 2023, arXiv:2307.14854. Grid pursuit-evasion as an autocurriculum generator. *Breach:* grid-native + autocurriculum-native comparison point.

## 3. Artificial-life & ecosystem RL
- **Neural MMO** — Suarez et al. 2019 (arXiv:1903.00784 *verify*); v1.3 arXiv:2001.12004; 2.0 arXiv:2311.03736. 128+ persistent agents foraging/fighting over resources; 2.0 adds flexible task/reward system. *Breach:* canonical "many animals living in one persistent world" platform.
- **Evolution of a Complex Predator-Prey Ecosystem on Large-scale Multi-Agent Deep RL** — Yamada, Shawe-Taylor, Fountas 2020, arXiv:2002.03267. Adds mating/reproduction → populations rise/fall endogenously; reproduces real-world-like dynamics classical ODE models can't. *Breach:* how to turn RL agents into a self-sustaining population (birth/death) — the bridge from "two trained animals" to "a living ecosystem."
- **Co-Evolution of Predator-Prey Ecosystems by RL** — Park et al. 2021, Entropy 23(4):461, doi:10.3390/e23040461. Iterated best-response co-evolution → genuine Lotka–Volterra population cycles. *Breach:* validation target ("do our trained animals cycle like real ones?").
- **Million-agent AI Population Dynamics** — Yang et al. 2017, arXiv:1709.04511. Group size/resource abundance shape grouping and aggression. *Breach:* scale precedent for population-level emergent phenomena on a grid.
- **Emergence of Complex Behavior in Large-Scale Ecological Environments** — Bejjani et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.18221. 60,000+ evolved-NN agents, NO explicit reward (reproduction/mutation/selection only); emergent long-range resource extraction, vision-based foraging, predation; **some behaviors only appear above a scale threshold**, larger worlds give more stable evolution. *Breach:* strongest recent argument for Breach's parallel-scale bet; template for reward-free evolutionary emergence.
- **Tuning Agent-Based Predator-Prey toward Lotka-Volterra** — 2026, arXiv:2606.13639 (*verify*). Calibration recipe for ecologically-plausible aggregate dynamics.
- **PolyWorld** — Yaeger 1994. Vision-input, embodied, evolving agents self-organize ecological behavior. *Breach:* spiritual ancestor.

## 4. Open-endedness
- **POET** — Wang et al. (Uber AI) 2019, arXiv:1901.01753; Enhanced POET 2020, arXiv:2003.08536. Co-generates environments AND agents; endless curriculum + specialists. *Breach:* blueprint for procedurally co-evolving the level/scenario alongside the animal; Breach's deterministic level authoring + parallelism suits a POET-style population of (environment, agent) pairs.
- **Novelty Search (Abandoning Objectives)** — Lehman & Stanley 2011, Evolutionary Computation 19(2). Reward behavioral novelty, ignore the objective — often outperforms objective-based search. *Breach:* for "surprise me with animal behaviors," optimize a novelty/behavior descriptor over grid occupancy/trajectory.
- **MAP-Elites** — Mouret & Clune 2015, arXiv:1504.04909. Quality-Diversity: best solution in every behavioral-descriptor cell → a repertoire of diverse-yet-good behaviors in one run. *Breach:* a library of animal "personalities"/gaits/strategies (descriptors = speed, aggression, cover-use). Directly gives "wild AND tame, many varied animals."
- **QDax** — Lim et al. 2023, arXiv:2308.03665. JAX/GPU-accelerated MAP-Elites. *Breach:* run QD on-GPU alongside a GPU-resident sim.
- **Minimal Criterion Coevolution (MCC)** — Brant & Stanley 2017 (GECCO). Two coevolving populations under only a minimal criterion → open-ended diversity, no novelty archive. *Breach:* elegantly cheap open-ended engine — coevolve hazards/levels vs animals with only survival criteria.
- **XLand (Open-Ended Learning → Generally Capable Agents)** — DeepMind 2021, arXiv:2107.12808. 700k games, 4k procedural worlds, frontier-tracking task distribution → zero-shot novel games. *Breach:* industrial evidence that procedural task/world distribution + frontier-tracking yields general robust behavior.
- **Lenia / Flow-Lenia** — Chan 2019+ (arXiv:1812.05433; Flow-Lenia arXiv:2506.08569; QD-in-Lenia arXiv:2406.04235). Continuous CA lifelike creatures. *Breach:* tangential; source of behavior-descriptor ideas + what open-ended emergence looks like.

## 5. Collective / flocking via RL
- **SELFish (Emergent Escape-based Flocking)** — Durve et al. 2019, arXiv:1905.04077. Self-interested agents rewarded ONLY to survive a predator → Boids-like flocking, no flocking reward. *Breach:* exactly the "prey learn to react to danger → flock" story, needs only a predator + survival signal.
- **Learning to Flock in Open Space** — 2025, arXiv:2506.15587. Cohesive flocking via MARL (centralized vs concurrent compared). *Breach:* clean credit-assignment choices for cohesion.
- **High-Performance Simulation for Scalable MARL (Boids)** — 2022, arXiv:2207.03945. Reynolds-Boids scaling to 1k–10k+ learners.
- **Selfish herd via MARL** — "A Selfish Herd with a Target" 2024, arXiv:2410.13167. Agents minimizing individual predation risk (domain-of-danger / Voronoi area) self-organize into swirls. *Breach:* a principled ethology-grounded reward (minimize your own domain of danger) → herding.
- **Fish schooling via MARL** — Wang et al. 2022, Ocean Engineering. Schooling from minimizing neighbor-loss rate. *Breach:* another minimal biologically-motivated reward, cheap on a grid.

## 6. Danger / threat response & fear learning
- **Evolution of Fear and Social Rewards in Prey-Predator Relationship** — Kanagawa & Doya (OIST) 2025, arXiv:2507.09992. *(Most on-theme recent paper.)* Prey and predator co-evolve innate reward functions (incl. a fear-like term) while learning behavior via RL. Findings: (1) social reward for seeing conspecifics evolves FIRST; (2) fear-of-predator evolves only AFTER social reward exists; (3) stronger predators → stronger evolved fear; (4) **fear did NOT evolve for static non-lethal pitfalls — only actively-hunting predators drove it.** *Breach:* almost turnkey — evolve the reward function itself (fear/social terms) on top of RL behavior, in a distributed sim. Read in full.
- **Combating RL's Sisyphean Curse with Intrinsic Fear** — Lipton et al. 2016, arXiv:1611.01211. A supervised fear model predicts imminent catastrophe; its output penalizes the Q-objective. *Breach:* lightweight, deterministic-sim-friendly durable danger-avoidance without reward hacking.
- **Risk-sensitive / distributional RL** — arXiv:2212.14743; arXiv:2307.01708. Optimize risk measures (CVaR, tail). *Breach:* a temperament knob — risk-averse prey vs risk-seeking foragers.
- **Neuroscience grounding** — basolateral amygdala threat conditioning (PMC10604397; PMC9245701); Pavlovian fear fits probabilistic-learning models (pcbi.1006243). *Breach:* if Erik wants physiologically-interpretable fear, these give the target phenomena to reproduce/validate.

## 7. Curiosity / intrinsic motivation
- **ICM** — Pathak et al. 2017, arXiv:1705.05363. Intrinsic reward = forward-model prediction error in a learned inverse-dynamics feature space. *Breach:* default "let animals explore the ship"; well-behaved on [C,H,W] planes.
- **RND** — Burda et al. 2018, arXiv:1810.12894. Bonus = error predicting a fixed random network's features; trivial to implement. *Breach:* cheapest robust novelty bonus to bolt on.
- **Pseudo-counts** — Bellemare et al. 2016, arXiv:1606.01868. Visitation pseudo-counts from a density model. *Breach:* on a discrete grid, TRUE counts per cell are essentially free and deterministic — use exact count bonuses (the tabular ideal).

## 8. Domestication / taming
- **TAMER / Deep TAMER / COACH** — Knox & Stone 2009; Deep TAMER 2018 arXiv:1709.10163. Human trainer supplies +/- signals; agent learns a human-reward model. *Breach:* the direct "tamer" mechanism (approval/disapproval → shaped behavior); clicker training analogue; deterministic replay makes sessions reproducible.
- **Cultural Transmission without Human Data (MEDAL-ADR)** — DeepMind CGI Team 2022, arXiv:2203.00715. Agents follow/imitate an expert in real time, RETAIN the skill after the expert leaves (other-agent attention loss, expert dropout, ADR). *Breach:* strongest recipe for taming-as-following; expert-dropout is key so tamed behavior survives the tamer's absence.
- **GAIL** — Ho & Ermon 2016, arXiv:1606.03476 (*verify*). Occupancy-matching via adversarial discriminator-as-reward. *Breach:* imitate a demonstrator without designing a taming reward; caveat: sample-inefficient/unstable (parallel deterministic rollouts help).
- **Zero-Shot LLMs replacing human feedback** — 2025, arXiv:2503.22723. *Breach:* scale taming feedback beyond a live human.

## 9. RL as a model of real animal behavior
- **Prefrontal Cortex as a Meta-RL System** — Wang et al. 2018, Nature Neuroscience 21:860 (biorxiv 295964). A recurrent RL agent trained across a task distribution develops a second faster RL process in its activations. *Breach:* principled route to animals that ADAPT within an episode — train recurrent policies over a task distribution to generalize to novel dangers/foods on the fly; ties intrinsic-fear + meta-learning into one interpretable story.
- Foundational grounding: RL↔dopamine-TD; optimal foraging; review arXiv:2007.01099. *Breach:* validation targets (marginal-value-theorem patch-leaving, TD-like fear acquisition).

## Recommended starting points for Breach (ranked)
1. **First predator–prey co-training (do first):** "Aquarium/simple_tag on real Breach physics" — N slower predators, few faster prey, species-shared policy weights, PPO or MADDPG, stabilized with Bansal-style opponent sampling from a frozen zoo. Success: predators discover encircling/cornering; prey discover evasion/cover-use.
2. **First danger-response / fear:** prey survival-only reward vs a predator → flocking/selfish-herd emerges for free (SELFish; "minimize domain of danger"). Add Intrinsic Fear so lethal-cell avoidance is durable. For evolved, ordered fear, replicate Kanagawa & Doya 2025.
3. **First taming:** Deep TAMER / COACH (scalar approval = clicker training) for one animal + human/scripted tamer; deterministic replay makes sessions reproducible. Layer MEDAL-ADR cultural transmission with expert-dropout so behavior sticks.
4. **First "wild, varied animals" generator:** RND or exact grid count-bonuses for exploration, then MAP-Elites / QDax to illuminate a repertoire of temperaments (speed, aggression, cover-use, risk).
5. **When ready for a living ecology:** reproduction/death (arXiv:2002.03267) for endogenous population dynamics, then a POET or MCC loop co-evolving levels/hazards vs animals. The scale finding (arXiv:2510.18221): push population/world size — complexity and stability are functions of scale (Breach's compute advantage).

## Key open problems / cautions
- **Co-adaptation instability (Red-Queen cycles & forgetting):** budget a versioned opponent pool from the start (cheap under determinism); exploiters/league if cycling.
- **Reward design for open-ended behavior is the science:** objective-based search is deceptive (Lehman & Stanley); prefer minimal/survival criteria + novelty/QD descriptors; Kanagawa & Doya's evolved-reward sidesteps hand-design.
- **Fear needs the RIGHT danger:** it evolved only vs actively-hunting predators, only AFTER social reward — ordering & threat-type matter.
- **Emergence can require scale:** small-scale null results may be false negatives — validate interesting claims at full parallel scale.
- **Determinism is a genuine asset — guard it:** ensure intrinsic-reward density models, novelty archives, any CPU-side RNG don't leak nondeterminism into the sim path (cf. the X-ARCH spawn-stat RNG finding).
- **Imitation/taming instability:** GAIL sample-inefficient; cultural-transmission collapses without expert-dropout.
- **Partial observability:** prey behind walls unobserved — predators may need a learned evader-prediction head (OPEN) or recurrent/meta-RL memory.
