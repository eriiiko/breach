# Smoke & Gas Medium Rendering — Technique Search for Breach ("B2 smoke honesty")

*Research pass, 2026-07-21. Scope: the rendered look of the smoke/gas medium itself — sub-tile detail, opacity/tonal mapping, per-species identity, edge preservation, mottle, and prior art in 2D games. Explicitly **out of scope** (covered by `docs/fire_rendering_research.md`, 2026-07-11): fire color/blackbody, fire particles, god-rays/light transport — the volumetric ray-marched light engine with per-gas absorption + scatter is treated as DONE and is only referenced as an input buffer here. Cost tags relative to a 2D tile grid: **cheap** = per-pixel arithmetic / a few texture fetches in an existing pass; **medium** = an extra buffer pass or feedback texture; **expensive** = fights the frame budget or touches the deterministic sim. Per standing owner preference, bloom is never proposed; every "glow" below is the existing inscatter buffer, not a post filter.*

Current baseline being replaced: one flat-grey premultiplied overlay, density → alpha through a gamma dial, constant RGB tint, bilinearly stretched 1 tile → 24 px.

---

## 1. Executive summary — top 3 changes, ranked

**① Advect a detail-noise layer along the real wind field (Q1).** The single biggest jump from "grey blur" to "plume." The entire film/games lineage for this — Max–Becker moving textures → [Neyret's Advected Textures](https://inria.hal.science/inria-00537472) → [Valve's flow maps in L4D2/Portal 2](https://alex.vlachos.com/graphics/Vlachos-SIGGRAPH10-WaterFlow.pdf) → [Wavelet Turbulence](http://www.tkim.graphics/WTURB/) — exists to fake a velocity field that Breach *has for real*. Two phase-offset tiling-fBm layers, UVs advected by the tile wind, crossfaded on a fixed latency with per-pixel phase jitter, used to erode/modulate the density alpha. Valve shipped this on ps2.0b-era hardware at +2 fetches / +21 ALU; Neyret's demo magnified a 64² grid to 512² output — almost exactly our 256²-grid-at-24-px/tile regime. **Cheap**, render-layer only, and the motion is *honest* because the advecting field is the real compressible solver.

**② One physically-mixed medium pass: Beer–Lambert alpha over summed species, composited as premultiplied (inscatter, transmittance) in HDR before ACES (Q2+Q3).** Replace `alpha = f(density)^gamma` with optical depth: `τ = Σ_species k_s·ρ_s`, `alpha = 1 − exp(−τ)` ([pbr-book, Transmittance](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Volume_Scattering/Transmittance)). Tint becomes the extinction-weighted mix of per-species albedos — soot near-black, steam white — so *all five gases live in one draw with no inter-species order question at all* (extinction sums; layered draws are both wrong and slower — [Frostbite's unified volumetrics](https://www.ea.com/frostbite/news/physically-based-unified-volumetric-rendering-in-frostbite) merges every medium into one extinction volume for exactly this reason). Compositing: premultiplied-over *is* the volume rendering operator `C = inscatter + T·background` — put the god-ray/`smoke_glow` result in RGB and `1−T` in alpha and the "alpha plume vs additive god-ray layer, which first?" question dissolves into a single blend. Must happen in linear HDR *before* ACES; alpha-blending after tone-map is precisely what makes smoke read as a flat grey film. **Cheap** — it's a curve change plus a draw-order/packing change.

**③ Sampling honesty at 24×: bicubic + noise domain-warp; defer MacCormack with an explicit revisit trigger (Q4).** Bilinear at 24× magnification prints the tile lattice (diamond stars) into every plume; Catmull-Rom/bicubic via 4 bilinear taps ([GPU Gems 2 ch. 20](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-iii-high-quality-rendering/chapter-20-fast-third-order-texture-filtering)) plus a small noise domain-warp of the sampling UV erases it for **cheap**. On MacCormack/BFECC: the evidence says it *is* visible at our plume scales (the BFECC papers demo on grids as coarse as 41×101), but it fixes a *different* failure (bulk transport: peak-density loss, silhouette diffusion) than the one currently on screen (missing sub-tile frequencies — which no advection scheme can add, since they're above grid Nyquist). Verdict: the "defer MacCormack, do render-side" lean is supported — with the trigger written down: if plumes still *fade/melt while traveling* after ①–③, that's transport dissipation, and only a sim-side scheme (deterministic-friendly, but golden re-baseline) fixes it.

## 2. Per-question findings

### Q1 — Sub-tile detail synthesis for a coarse density field *(top priority)*

**The lineage, and what each rung adds:**
- **Max & Becker 1995 (Flow Visualization Using Moving Textures)** — the root idea: advect a noise texture's UVs along the flow; since the parameterization stretches forever, periodically *regenerate* it, and hide resets by blending **three textures phase-shifted by 2π/3** so total weight stays constant (each weight `⅓(1−cos(2π·t/τ))`). Neyret notes the illusion needs the pattern to travel ≳1 noise pseudo-period per cycle (~10× in practice) — i.e. don't make the latency too short. *(Cited via Neyret 2003 and Vlachos 2010, both of which build directly on it.)*
- **[Neyret, "Advected Textures" (SCA 2003)](https://inria.hal.science/inria-00537472)** — the production-quality version. (a) A single global latency can't serve fast and slow regions at once, so keep **N layers with different latencies** and pick per-location by *accumulated deformation*: integrate the strain-tensor norm ‖ε‖ (ε = ½(∇v+∇vᵀ)) per texture since its last reset, and blend the two layers bracketing a user target deformation — "temporal MIP-mapping." (b) **Blending fix**: naively lerping turbulence textures causes ghosting/contrast loss; instead blend the *base noise octaves* linearly, then apply the nonlinear turbulence (e.g. `|2b−1|` sum) and color shader once on the blended spectrum. (c) Small scales get *flow-noise* animation with gradient-rotation rate proportional to the local **vorticity** — small scales visibly swirl where the real flow swirls. Demo: 64×64 grid → 512×512 frames, real-time with baked noise textures in 2003.
- **[Perlin & Neyret, "Flow Noise" (SIGGRAPH 2001 sketch)](https://mrl.cs.nyu.edu/~perlin/flownoise-talk/)** ([paper page](https://morpho.inrialpes.fr/Publications/2001/PN01/)) — animate noise *as if it were fluid*: rotate the Perlin gradient vectors over time and pseudo-advect small octaves by the large ones (`T(x)=N(x)+N(2x)/2+N(4x)/4` with per-octave phase pushed by the lower octave). A [tiling 2D/3D implementation exists in JCGT](https://www.jcgt.org/published/0011/01/02/paper-lowres.pdf).
- **[Vlachos, "Water Flow in Portal 2 / L4D2" (SIGGRAPH 2010)](https://alex.vlachos.com/graphics/Vlachos-SIGGRAPH10-WaterFlow.pdf)** — the shipped-game distillation, and the recipe to copy first. Flow map at ~4 texels/meter (≈ per-tile vectors — exactly Breach's wind-field granularity); UV offset = flow vector × time; **distortion only looks good for the first ~⅓ of UV space** (their restatement of Max–Becker), so keep accumulated offset under that; **two layers offset by half a phase**, crossfaded to hide each restart; a **half-texture UV offset between the layers** kills repetition; **a per-pixel noise value added to the blend phase** kills the whole-screen "pulse" of synchronized resets. Cost on 2010 hardware: +2 texture fetches, +21 ALU vs plain scrolling. For color (not normal) content they center the distortion interval on zero (−f…+f) so the blend peak sits at the undistorted position.
- **[Kim, Thürey, James, Gross — "Wavelet Turbulence" (SIGGRAPH 2008)](http://www.tkim.graphics/WTURB/)** ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1399504.1360649)) — the film-grade up-res: measure what high-frequency energy the coarse sim is missing, then synthesize incompressible band-limited noise weighted **2^(−5/6) per octave** (Kolmogorov spectrum), keeping temporal coherence by — again — advected, periodically-regenerated texture coordinates. Ran as a *post-process* on solves as coarse as 50³, magnified 8×. Two takeaways for Breach: render-time detail synthesis is the industry norm even at film quality, and ~0.56/octave is the physically-flavored fBm persistence.
- **[van Wijk, Image-Based Flow Visualization (SIGGRAPH 2002)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image-based_flow_visualization)** — the stateful alternative: a feedback buffer where each frame you warp the previous noise image by the velocity field and blend in a fraction β of fresh noise. Streak length ≈ 1/β frames; no explicit regeneration cycle ever needed.

**Concrete recipe for Breach** *(cheap; all in the existing overlay pass at screen resolution — do not bake a 6144² world texture)*:
1. Bake one tiling fBm noise texture (~256², 3–4 octaves, persistence ~0.5–0.56), plus a small static jitter-noise texture.
2. In the smoke shader: two UV sets `uv_i = p·f_base − wind(p)·(t − t0_i)·k`, phases offset by τ/2; crossfade weight `w = ½−½cos(2π t/τ)` plus the per-pixel jitter-phase offset (Vlachos); half-texture offset between the layers. Base noise wavelength ~2–4 tiles, so at 24 px/tile the octaves span ~96 px down to ~6 px.
3. Choose τ from the ⅓ rule: `τ ≈ (L_noise/3)/v_typical` — with a 4-tile noise period and ~0.5 tile/s drift, τ ≈ 2–3 s; gustier maps want shorter. (Neyret's adaptive-latency layers are the upgrade path if one τ can't serve both a breach-blast jet and a lazy ceiling pool; start with one τ + jitter and only add the second latency if fast regions visibly smear.)
4. Use the noise to **erode alpha** (Nubis-style remap of optical depth by noise, strongest where density is low → wispy edges, solid cores) and to modulate tint a few percent (see Q5). Macro shape stays 100% the sim's density field.
5. Optional (**medium**): curl-noise micro-swirl added to the sampled wind before advecting UVs (Bridson 2007, already adopted for particles in the fire doc), and/or flow-noise gradient rotation scaled by |curl(wind)| per Neyret — makes the sub-tile detail *rotate* where the real field rotates.
6. Variant (**medium**): IBFV-style feedback buffer (ping-pong render texture, warp-by-wind + β fresh noise) — raylib-friendly, gives true streaklines, but is stateful (history buffer, resize/reset handling). Start with the stateless 2-layer cycle; it's deterministic-replay-friendly for free if `t` is derived from sim tick count rather than wall clock.

**Maps onto Breach:** this is the fire doc's "don't simulate detail — advect it" principle applied to the smoke medium itself, and it's the one place our stack is *strictly better positioned* than every cited system: Valve authored flow maps in Houdini and Neyret ran a toy solver; Breach's flow map is the live compressible wind field, so detail licks downwind, curls at doorways, and jets through breaches with zero authoring.

### Q2 — Density→opacity, tonal mapping, and compositing with an HDR/ACES scene

- **Opacity curve** *(cheap)* — the physical mapping is Beer–Lambert: transmittance `T = exp(−τ)` with optical depth `τ = k·ρ·ds` ([pbr-book 4ed, Transmittance](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Volume_Scattering/Transmittance); [3ed, Volume Scattering Processes](https://pbr-book.org/3ed-2018/Volume_Scattering/Volume_Scattering_Processes)). So `alpha = 1 − exp(−k·ρ)`: linear in thin smoke (every wisp visible, slope k), saturating in thick — the behavior the current gamma dial is approximating by hand. Keep an artistic remap if wanted, but apply it in **τ-space** (`τ' = a·τ^b`), not on alpha, so thick-smoke saturation stays honest. *Maps onto Breach: replaces the density→alpha gamma dial one-for-one.*
- **Thin smoke: banding, not color, is the failure mode** *(cheap)* — large soft low-alpha gradients band in 8-bit; the canonical fix is dithering, per Playdead's INSIDE talk ([GDC 2016, "Low Complexity, High Fidelity"](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023002/Low-Complexity-High-Fidelity-INSIDE), [slides repo](https://github.com/playdeadgames/publications/tree/master/INSIDE)): a little noise saves subtle art "from drowning in color-banding." Blue-noise dither the overlay (or keep it 16F until composite). Q1's erosion noise incidentally masks most banding too.
- **Premultiplied alpha is correct here, and for two more reasons than we're currently using** *(cheap)* — (a) filtering: straight-alpha bilinear interpolation mixes in the colors of transparent texels and dark-fringes every wispy edge; premultiplied interpolation is exactly correct — blending scaled images equals scaling blended images only under premultiplication ([Íñigo Quílez](https://iquilezles.org/articles/premultipliedalpha/), [Real-Time Rendering blog](https://www.realtimerendering.com/blog/gpus-prefer-premultiplication/)). (b) unification: with premultiplied `(RGB, A)`, `A=0` with nonzero RGB is *pure additive* — over-composited smoke and additive glow can share one buffer and one blend state.
- **Integration with HDR/ACES — the load-bearing finding** *(cheap, ordering only)* — single-scatter volume compositing is `C = C_inscatter + T·C_background`; real-time volumetrics (Frostbite froxels) accumulate exactly an (inscatter, transmittance) pair per pixel and composite it that way ([Hillaire, SIGGRAPH 2015](https://www.ea.com/frostbite/news/physically-based-unified-volumetric-rendering-in-frostbite), [course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/)). For Breach that means: build **one premultiplied layer with RGB = the ray-marched `smoke_glow`/inscatter result and alpha = 1−T from summed species optical depths**, and draw it over the linear-HDR world in a single premult-over blend *before* ACES. This answers "alpha plume vs additive god-ray layer, which order?" — neither: they are the two halves of one operator, and packing them together makes double-attenuation (smoke dimming its own glow) impossible by construction. All of it must precede tone-mapping — transparencies belong in the linear HDR stage of the pipeline ([pipeline-order references](https://www.renderingevolution.net/?p=103), [linear HDR pipeline design](https://kosmonautblog.wordpress.com/2017/03/26/designing-a-linear-hdr-pipeline/)); blending a grey overlay onto an already-ACES-mapped image is the classic "smoke looks like a film on the lens" bug, and matches the current baseline's symptoms.
- **What films/games actually ship:** film = full radiative transfer, path-traced ([Production Volume Rendering course, SIGGRAPH 2017](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3084873.3084907)); AAA real-time = the froxel (S, T) pair above; everything lighter (including basically all 2D games) = artist alpha curves on sprites. The (S, T)-pair model is the shippable middle and Breach already computes both halves.

### Q3 — Per-species visual identity in one composite

- **The core result: species are mixed *inside* the medium, not layered draws** *(cheap)* — extinction is additive across constituents: `τ_total = Σ_s k_s·ρ_s`, and the medium's body color is the extinction-weighted albedo `A_mix = Σ_s A_s·k_s·ρ_s / τ_total` (heterogeneous-media formulation, [pbr-book](https://pbr-book.org/3ed-2018/Volume_Scattering/Volume_Scattering_Processes); [Frostbite](https://www.ea.com/frostbite/news/physically-based-unified-volumetric-rendering-in-frostbite) unifies all participating media into one extinction+albedo volume per froxel for the same reason). Consequence: **there is no inter-species blend order** — order only exists if each species gets its own draw call, which is physically wrong (over-compositing is order-dependent; a mixed gas is not) and costs more. One pass, five multiply-adds.
- **Identity dials per species** *(cheap)* — two numbers each, `k_s` (how fast it goes opaque per unit density) and albedo `A_s`, plus how much of its look comes from the *lit* half (inscatter RGB) vs the *extinction* half (alpha × dark body color):
  - **soot/black_smoke**: large `k`, near-black albedo — physically soot is a strong absorber, so a little soot should dominate `A_mix` and visibly blacken a steam mix (this is the render-side hookup for the fire doc's "oxygen-starvation smoke story" pick ②). Its identity lives in the alpha/extinction half; it *kills* glow rather than carrying it.
  - **steam/white_smoke**: moderate `k`, near-white albedo — its identity lives almost entirely in the inscatter half: brilliant near lights (the ray-marcher's scatter already does this), neutral grey in shadow. Do not give steam an emissive floor; if steam glows in a dark room the Aliens read (Q5) is lost.
  - **poison / teargas**: mid `k`, hue carried in **albedo** (desaturated sickly green-yellow / pale warm grey), never in an additive term — a hue in albedo still reads in shadow and under colored light, an additive tint reads as neon. A tiny emissive floor for gameplay legibility is a legitimate *design* override — flag it as such, not physics.
  - **fuel_gas**: essentially transparent — near-zero `k`, no albedo; give it identity as *refraction*: use its density to drive a small screen-space domain-warp of the already-rendered scene (heat-haze shimmer). **Cheap–medium** (needs the scene grab, which the render-texture pipeline already has).
- **Alpha weighting between species** is therefore not a stylistic knob but the `k_s` vector — tune it so the *crossover points* feel right (how much soot turns a white plume grey, how much steam whitens a black one). *Maps onto Breach: the per-gas absorption/scatter_albedo table that already drives the light engine is the same data — read the render-side `k_s`, `A_s` from it so the plume body and its god-rays can never disagree about what a gas is.*

### Q4 — Filament/edge preservation: MacCormack/BFECC vs render-side

- **What sim-side correction buys, with evidence at our scale** — BFECC ([Kim, Liu, Rossignac — FlowFixer](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221314833_FlowFixer_using_BFECC_for_fluid_simulation); [TVCG version](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6704642_Advections_with_Significantly_Reduced_Dissipation_and_Diffusion)) demonstrates on a **41×101 smoke grid** that plain semi-Lagrangian visibly bleeds density away while BFECC keeps the plume at full density — i.e. at 5–20-cell plume widths the difference is *not* subtle, it shows as plumes that fade and fatten while traveling. [Selle et al.'s MacCormack](https://physbam.stanford.edu/papers/stanford2006-09.pdf) gets the same second-order accuracy from two advection sweeps plus a correction (≈2× advection cost, no third sweep, unconditionally stable with a min/max limiter). Numerical-dissipation reduction remains an active thread precisely because SL smearing is the dominant coarse-grid artifact ([Huang et al. 2015 survey/intro](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/huang15reducing/huang15reducing.pdf)).
- **What it cannot buy** — frequencies above grid Nyquist. A corrected 256² field is still 256²; at 24 px/tile the screen shows ~24× more resolution than any advection scheme can put into the data. The missing wisps/filaments the eye complains about first are *sub-tile*, and only synthesis (Q1) adds those — which is exactly the premise of [Wavelet Turbulence](http://www.tkim.graphics/WTURB/) (coarse solve carries large scales; detail is synthesized at up-res time).
- **Render-side toolkit** *(all cheap)*:
  - **Bicubic sampling of the density texture** via 4 bilinear taps ([Sigg & Hadwiger, GPU Gems 2 ch. 20](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-iii-high-quality-rendering/chapter-20-fast-third-order-texture-filtering); [fewer-taps variants](https://vec3.ca/posts/bicubic-filtering-in-fewer-taps)). B-spline is the smooth/blurring member — good for the alpha body; Catmull-Rom keeps more edge snap. Point sampling is out (tile mosaics); plain bilinear at 24× is the current lattice-diamond artifact.
  - **Noise domain-warp of the sampling UV** (a few px, from Q1's advected noise) — hides any residual lattice and turns smooth contours into wisps.
  - **Contrast/remap in τ-space** (`τ' = a·τ^b`, b>1) — re-steepens edges that grid diffusion softened; the render analog of sharpening, and reversible per-frame. Erosion-remap at low densities (Nubis-style) makes edges *ragged* rather than merely steeper.
  - Uploading a pre-noised higher-res density texture is strictly dominated by doing the same math in-shader (same result, more bandwidth, no new information) — skip.
- **Verdict for Breach:** the lean is supported — **do render-side now** (it targets the artifact actually visible at 24 px/tile, costs nothing, and can't touch determinism), **defer MacCormack** but write down its trigger: *if, after B2, plumes measurably lose peak density / silhouette while traveling across a room, that is transport dissipation — no render trick recovers it, and the fix is a sim-side MacCormack/BFECC step* (integer-friendly: two SL sweeps + a correction + clamp — deterministic in Q16.16, but it's a behavioral change: digest/golden re-baseline, HUMAN-TEST gate, written rationale).

### Q5 — Mottle/speckle ("dirty plume") treatments

- **Two families** — (a) *pure value-noise mottle*: multiply albedo/alpha by an fBm channel; this is what 2D/stylized games ship (the [Diablo 3 smoke recreation](https://realtimevfx.com/t/tutorial-how-to-recreate-the-smoke-effect-from-diablo-3-in-unity-using-shader-graph-and-visual-effect-graph/12579) is layered UV-distorted noise end-to-end). (b) *physically-seeded mottle*: scale mottle amplitude and darkness by the **real soot fraction** — dirty lumps appear only where chemistry made soot. Breach can do (b) for the cost of (a); no cited engine can, because for everyone else soot is paint. **Cheap** — reuses Q1's noise fetches.
- **Cloud-craft transcription** *(cheap)* — the Nubis/Decima recipe is base-shape noise **remap-eroded by higher-frequency Worley at edges** (base Perlin-Worley + three increasing-frequency Worley channels, separate small detail texture; [Guerrilla's Nubis](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-authoring-real-time-volumetric-cloudscapes-with-the-decima-engine), parameters documented in the open [Meteoros reimplementation](https://github.com/AmanSachan1/Meteoros)). 2D transcription: erode τ hardest where density is low (wispy boundary), and darken albedo where density is high *and* detail-noise is low — sooty internal lumps, the flat-shaded cousin of the clouds' dark-edge "powder" look.
- **Rules from the reference look** — the *Aliens* (1986) escape-pod/atmosphere haze is dense practical steam with hard backlight; on set, smoke plus "shifting shadows" was explicitly the concealer/atmosphere tool ([Oseman's Aliens breakdown](https://neiloseman.com/10-clever-camera-tricks-in-aliens/); the film's effects stack was in-camera layering, [filmsite](https://www.filmsite.org/visualeffects13.html)). The structure the eye reads there is **lighting structure, not albedo speckle** — bright shafts and internal shadow drift, on an almost featureless white medium. Translation: steam mottle stays low-frequency, ≲10% amplitude, and steam's visual interest comes from the inscatter buffer + slow Q1 drift; reserve high-frequency (~≤6 px) speckle for soot-heavy, high-τ cores. And never static: mottle must ride the Q1 advection or it reads as a screen overlay — the current flat-tint failure mode generalized.

### Q6 — 2D games with genuinely great smoke

- **Noita** ([GDC 2019, "Exploring the Tech and Design of Noita"](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025695/Exploring-the-Tech-and-Design); [every-pixel-simulated framing](https://noitagame.com/)) — gases are cellular-automata pixels in the Falling Everything engine: smoke rises, pools into pockets under ceilings, gradually decays ([wiki behavior page](https://noita.wiki.gg/wiki/Smoke)). Shading is nearly flat translucent pixels; the smoke looks alive because **motion is honest and edge raggedness is native** — crucially, Noita's sim resolution *is* its render resolution, so it never faces our 24× magnification gap. Lesson, not recipe: Breach can't (and shouldn't — determinism, cost) per-pixel-sim the gas, but Q1's wind-advected noise is precisely the way to buy Noita's "honest motion" at sub-tile scale from a coarse field.
- **INSIDE** ([GDC 2016](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023002/Low-Complexity-High-Fidelity-INSIDE); [80.lv summary](https://80.lv/articles/the-rendering-of-inside-low-complexity-high-fidelity)) — 2.5D, and the strongest atmosphere-per-technique budget in games: few, perfectly-behaved layers; dithering everywhere against banding; restrained fog wedges. Direct steals: dither the thin-smoke gradients (Q2); prefer one well-behaved composite over stacking effect layers (Q2/Q3's single-pass design).
- **The 2D industry default, for contrast** — flipbook or single-frame noise sprites with UV distortion/flow ([realtimevfx threads on plume construction](https://realtimevfx.com/t/unreal-engine-4-smoke-plume/4836), [approach discussion](https://realtimevfx.com/t/smoke-vfx-want-to-know-how-you-would-approach-solving-this/15374), Diablo 3 tutorial above): i.e., the industry's 2D answer *is* Q1's machinery driven by fake flow. Nothing found in 2D shipping games does per-species physical mixing or field-driven smoke color — that remains Breach-unique territory (consistent with the fire doc's pick ②).
- **Sci-vis cousin** — modern live wind maps use exactly the IBFV/advected-texture family over real vector fields ([IBFV overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image-based_flow_visualization)) — a reminder that this technique's whole purpose is *showing a real velocity field*, which is Breach's brief in one sentence.

## 3. Cross-cutting cautions

- **Keep noise time on the sim tick.** All of Q1/Q5 is render-layer, but if `t` in the advection cycles comes from wall clock, replays/spectators render different smoke. Deriving phase from tick count costs nothing and keeps visuals replay-stable.
- **Evaluate noise per screen pixel in the overlay shader** — never bake a world-resolution (up to 6144²) smoke texture.
- **Don't split the composite.** The moment steam, soot, and glow become separate draw calls, order bugs (double attenuation, glow-through-soot) return. One premultiplied (inscatter, 1−T) layer, drawn once, pre-ACES.
- **The sim/render boundary stays where it is.** Every recommendation above reads `wind`, `ρ_species`, `T`, and the existing inscatter buffer; none writes back. The single flagged exception is the Q4 MacCormack trigger, which is a deliberate, gated sim change if ever taken.
