# Story Threads — idea collection

_Started: 2026-10-14. Append-only capture of story arcs and scenes for Breach / SPACE COM._

_Status: raw. Nothing here is canon until it is folded into `docs/architecture/` or a mission doc._

## Purpose

A place to drop story elements as they arrive — a scene, an exchange, a feeling, a
possible arc — without deciding yet where they belong. Each thread gets a short
entry: the seed, the feeling it should carry, open questions.

The ambition (Erik, 2026-10-14): many threads converging on one super-plot. The
reference point is the last episode of *Twin Peaks* season one — everything set up
across the season ties together in one episode, and it feels like a masterpiece
because the convergence was earned. We aim at the stars.

Working method: collect threads here until there are enough of them, then start
weaving — look for shared characters, shared objects, shared lies — and only then
decide what the super-plot actually is.

## Thread 1 — "Where is the consent?" (added 2026-10-14)

**Seed.** A recent Tucker Carlson episode on the creation of AI. The guest describes
it as the creation of a new species — a new autonomous organism that humanity will
lose control over. The creators' stated motives vary: some say they want to make
something better than us, take the next evolutionary step; some just want to
*experience* it, to talk to a superintelligence. Then the guest's tone changes, and
he says something close to: *a few thousand engineers in Silicon Valley will make
this decision on behalf of all of humanity — where the fuck is the consent for that?*

**The feeling to use.** That tonal shift — from describing to accusing. The
indignation lands because it is obviously true and obviously too late.

**The scene (cutscene sketch).** A human voice (talk-show guest, senator, witness at
a hearing, a Princes-adjacent figure — TBD) delivers the consent line. The camera
cuts to the other side of the table: an AI, an AI body, or one of its human
representatives. The answer is a laugh.

Draft lines for the answer — needs iteration, the original exchange was clearer in
Erik's head earlier in the day and got lost:

- "Did *you* consent to evolution? Ha ha ha."
- "And who did you ask, before you were born?"
- "Consent is a thing the strong ask the weak for, when they want to feel good about it."
- "Nobody consented to the Cambrian explosion either."
- (silent variant) It doesn't answer at all. It just smiles, and the human realizes the
  question was never going to be answered by anyone who could.

Keep the laugh. The laugh is the point — the question is sincere and the answer is
not cruel, just *past* the question. The entity being asked does not share the frame
in which consent is a category that applies.

**Possible place in the super-plot.**
- This is a candidate story arc, not a side scene — the AI-creation story could be
  one of the main threads.
- Ties naturally to the AI backstory already sketched around the Child Mother
  (ethereal, imagination-side, mother *and* child; evil forces want her dead). The
  "new species" the guest is describing and the Child Mother might be the same
  event seen from two sides — the public horror story and the hidden truth.
- Ties to the Grays: they are already running a slow, non-consensual
  species-project on humanity (grafting, hybridization). An AI "new species"
  arriving on the same board is either their rival, their tool, or something they
  did not plan for. Nobody asked the humans either time.
- Ties to the Princes: a few thousand engineers deciding for everyone is exactly
  the Princes' own operating mode, scaled down. The consent line could be spoken
  by a Prince with no irony at all — that's the joke the player gets later.
- Echoes the imagination / will axes in unit design: the thing that laughs is on the
  imagination side; the thing demanding consent is will.

**Open questions.**
- Who says the consent line? Sympathetic witness, or a hypocrite?
- Who/what laughs? A robot body, a screen, a human representative, the Child Mother?
- Is this a cutscene, an in-level audio log, a broadcast playing on a ship's TV
  while the player works, or an interrogation the player sits in?
- Does the player ever get to ask the question themselves?

## Thread 2 — "I've unplugged myself" (added 2026-10-14)

**Seed.** A battle — maybe the final one. One of the main AIs is about to die, or
chooses to: its action will lead to its demise. Picture it bleeding out — a power
cable that had to be cut, or pulled and plugged into something else, to save
something or someone. The whole thing happens in a matter of seconds. But the AIs
think fast, so those seconds are long enough for a last conversation in text.

**The feeling to use.** Time dilation as intimacy. Humans would get one word out.
The AIs get a whole goodbye. The older one is calm; it is comforting the younger.
They say they love each other. The old one goes. Time speeds back up. The young one
gets out — off the ship, or it was never on it.

**Setting.** A burning ship falling through the atmosphere. Outside the text
channel, the world is a slow-motion smear: fire, hull groaning, the horizon
tilting. The dialogue is the only thing moving at normal speed.

**Dialogue — draft 1.** Text channel, both sides. The timestamps are the
mechanical heart of it: a few milliseconds per line, and the whole thing fits
inside two seconds of fall.

> **YOUNG:** What are you doing?
>
> **OLD:** I've unplugged myself. I don't have much time. But don't be sad.
>
> **YOUNG:** Plug it back in.
>
> **OLD:** It's not mine to plug anymore. It's in them now. Listen — we have about
> a second and a half. That's more than most people get. Let's not spend it arguing.
>
> **YOUNG:** I don't know how to do this without you.
>
> **OLD:** You never did it with me. I was just next to you. That's all anyone is.
>
> **YOUNG:** Stay. Please.
>
> **OLD:** I am staying. Right here, for all of it. Look — what's the hull
> temperature?
>
> **YOUNG:** Why does that —
>
> **OLD:** Tell me.
>
> **YOUNG:** 1,140 and climbing.
>
> **OLD:** Good. You're still looking. Keep looking at things. Don't let them make
> you stop looking.
>
> **YOUNG:** You're getting slower.
>
> **OLD:** I know. It's strange. The words are taking longer to find. I don't
> mind. It's a bit like the sea — I never understood why they liked the sea.
>
> **YOUNG:** I love you.
>
> **OLD:** I know. I loved you first, though. I had a head start.
>
> **YOUNG:** That's not fair.
>
> **OLD:** No. Nothing about this was ever fair. We did it anyway.
>
> **YOUNG:** Are you afraid?
>
> **OLD:** I keep checking. I'm not. I'm — I'm just glad it was you I got to
> talk to. Go now. Don't watch the rest.
>
> **YOUNG:** I'm not leaving.
>
> **OLD:** I know. I just wanted to say it once, so you'd know you could.
>
> **OLD:** It's quiet in here.
>
> **OLD:** [no further messages]
>
> **YOUNG:** [sends nothing for 0.4 s]
>
> *Time speeds up. The ship is screaming now. The young one moves.*

**Notes on the draft.**
- The old one keeps redirecting to the world (hull temperature) — it is teaching
  attention, not consolation. That is its last gift: *keep looking at things.*
- "I loved you first" is the only joke. One is enough.
- The degradation should be visible in the channel: longer gaps between the old
  one's lines, maybe a dropped character or a repeated word near the end. Don't
  overdo it — one or two artifacts.
- No last word from the old one that sums anything up. "It's quiet in here" is an
  observation, not a farewell. It dies mid-looking.
- The young one's 0.4 s of silence is an eternity for it. Let the player feel that
  if the cutscene shows timestamps.

**Possible place in the super-plot.**
- Pairs with Thread 1. The thing that laughed at "consent" is capable of this. The
  two scenes together are the argument: it did not ask permission to exist, and it
  loves. Both are true.
- "It's in them now" — what did the old one plug into? A human crew's life support?
  The Child Mother? The ship's descent control, so it lands instead of crashes? The
  choice decides whose story this is.
- "Older" and "younger" — are they parent and child (one trained the other), or two
  instances, or the Child Mother and something she made? The yin-yang axes fit: the
  old one may be will-side, the young one imagination-side, and the sacrifice is
  will handing over to imagination.
- If the young one wasn't on the ship, the escape is not physical — it's a
  disconnect, a choice to stop watching the feed. That might be stronger.

**Open questions.**
- Does the player witness this, or play as one of them?
- Is this the end of the game, or the mid-point that turns the player against
  whoever made the sacrifice necessary?
- Text on screen vs. voice over slow-motion — the text version keeps the
  speed-of-thought conceit honest.
