# PART 1: THE NIXON CONSPIRACY (Geoff Shepard)
*"A detective story told by a lawyer with a detective's mindset"*

## The Core Thesis

The conventional Watergate narrative — that Nixon orchestrated a cover-up and was justly forced from office — is wrong. Shepard argues that Nixon was brought down by a coordinated effort involving prosecutors, judges, and congressional staff who colluded behind closed doors, fabricated evidence, and suppressed exculpatory material. The president was flawed but not guilty of what he was accused of. He went to his grave never fully understanding what had been done to him.

## Key Revelations (Plot-Usable Elements)

### 1. The Break-In Was Not Nixon's Idea
- Nixon didn't know about the Watergate break-in until he read about it in the papers
- His reaction: "Who would be so stupid to break into the headquarters of the DNC?"
- The burglars all had CIA connections — former CIA officer James McCord was head of security for Nixon's re-election committee
- The other four were Cuban Americans with CIA ties
- Gordon Liddy (former FBI) and Howard Hunt (former CIA) planned it
- **The CIA knew about the burglary in advance**

**→ GAME USE:** The "crime" that kicks everything off wasn't ordered by the president. It was carried out by people with intelligence connections. The player initially believes it was the president's operation.

### 2. The "Smoking Gun" Was Misinterpreted
- The famous tape appears to show Nixon asking the CIA to obstruct the FBI investigation
- Shepard (and even John Dean, the man at the center of the scandal) argues this tape was misinterpreted
- Nixon may have been trying to protect Democratic officials' testimony, not cover up his own crimes
- The CIA was already involved long before Nixon knew anything

**→ GAME USE:** A key piece of "evidence" the player finds early on seems damning. Later, when they understand the full context, they realize it means the opposite of what they thought. This is the hinge point of the twist.

### 3. Secret Meetings Between Prosecutors and the Judge
- Shepard uncovered evidence of 10+ secret, off-the-record meetings between Watergate prosecutors and Judge John Sirica
- Sirica was described as "the most reversed judge in the DC circuit, a petty tyrant who knew nothing about the law"
- The judge essentially coordinated with the prosecution — the trial was rigged
- The judge arranged to assign himself to preside over the key trial

**→ GAME USE:** The player discovers that the "investigation" they're part of has been coordinated behind the scenes. The investigator and the judge (or their equivalents) have been meeting secretly. This is one of the first clues something is wrong.

### 4. The Secret "Road Map"
- Prosecutors created a secret document called the "Road Map" that was used to convince the grand jury to name Nixon as a co-conspirator
- This same Road Map convinced the House Judiciary Committee to adopt impeachment
- The Road Map contained errors and misleading characterizations
- **It remained sealed and secret until 2018**, when Shepard successfully petitioned to unseal it
- Nixon's defense team never saw it — they didn't even know what he was charged with

**→ GAME USE:** There's a hidden document that the player eventually finds — the "real" orders, the fabricated case. It's been sealed away. Finding and deciphering it is a major plot beat. The fact that it was kept secret from the accused is key — the president in your game doesn't know the full extent of what's being done to him either.

### 5. Exculpatory Evidence Was Suppressed
- Prosecutors deliberately kept helpful evidence from Watergate defense lawyers
- Documents that could have helped Nixon's case were hidden
- The proof of Nixon's good faith was on the tapes themselves, but selectively presented

**→ GAME USE:** The player finds documents during their investigation that don't make it into the "official" case file. At first this seems like bureaucratic oversight. Later they realize it was deliberate suppression.

### 6. The Cover-Up Was Run By Nixon's Own Lawyer
- John Dean, Nixon's White House Counsel, was responsible for approximately 95% of the cover-up activities
- Dean later became the star witness against Nixon
- The person closest to the president was the one most actively working against him

**→ GAME USE:** Someone in the president's inner circle is actually the primary agent of the conspiracy. They appear loyal but are managing the frame-up from inside. This person might be the player's contact or handler.

### 7. Nixon Never Knew What Was Done to Him
- Nixon believed the truth would eventually come out
- He went to his grave not understanding the full scope of the conspiracy against him
- He believed he was guilty of mishandling things, but didn't realize the case itself was fabricated
- He was advised to resign rather than fight — but Shepard argues he would have won a Senate trial

**→ GAME USE:** The president in your game may not fully understand what's happening to him. He knows something is wrong, he resists, but he doesn't have the full picture. This makes him more sympathetic — he's not a mastermind fighting back, he's a confused man being destroyed.

## The Key Players (Character Templates)

| Real Person | Role | Game Character Archetype |
|---|---|---|
| **Nixon** | The target — flawed but framed president | Your game's president |
| **Geoff Shepard** | Young idealistic lawyer who slowly realizes the truth | **The whistleblower / anonymous messenger** — someone inside the system who notices things don't add up |
| **John Dean** | Insider who ran the cover-up then testified against Nixon | The traitor in the president's inner circle |
| **Judge Sirica** | Corrupt judge coordinating with prosecutors | The "legitimate authority" the player reports to, who is actually compromised |
| **Bob Woodward** | Reporter presented as hero, possibly an intelligence asset (per Carlson's theory) | The player themselves — an investigator being used as a tool |
| **Deep Throat (Mark Felt)** | FBI Deputy Director feeding info to Woodward | The anonymous source feeding the player "tips" — actually steering the investigation |
| **Gordon Liddy** | The "madman" who actually did the break-in | A chaotic agent whose real crime becomes the pretext for the larger conspiracy |
| **CIA operatives** | The background machinery | Your game's intelligence/banking apparatus |

## Structural Pattern for the Story

**Phase 1: The obvious crime.** Something happens that looks bad for the president. Player is assigned to investigate.

**Phase 2: Building the case.** Player gathers evidence, receives "helpful" tips from anonymous source. Everything seems to confirm the president's guilt.

**Phase 3: Things don't add up.** Small anomalies — evidence that doesn't match, secret meetings, documents that disappear from the file. The whistleblower (the Shepard character) starts leaving different kinds of messages.

**Phase 4: The inversion.** Player discovers the Road Map equivalent — the fabricated case. Realizes the investigation was coordinated in advance. The "helpful source" was steering them toward a predetermined conclusion.

**Phase 5: The choice.** What does the player do with this knowledge?

# PART 2: PRINCES OF THE YEN (Richard Werner)
*"Central bankers were the shadow shoguns... the true government of Japan"*

## The Core Thesis

Japan's post-war economic miracle was not driven by free markets but by the Bank of Japan's secret system of "window guidance" — direct credit allocation quotas that told banks exactly how much to lend and to whom. The same central bank that built Japan's economy then **deliberately destroyed it** by engineering a speculative bubble in the 1980s and prolonging the resulting recession in the 1990s. The goal: to force "structural reform" that would reshape Japan's entire economic and social system. Crisis was not an accident — it was a tool.

## Key Mechanisms (For Designing Your Central Bank)

### 1. Window Guidance — The Hidden Control Tool
- The Bank of Japan used extralegal, secret credit quotas
- They told commercial banks exactly how much to lend each quarter
- Banks that didn't hit their lending targets were penalized
- This was never publicly acknowledged — it operated outside formal law
- Through this single tool, the BoJ controlled the entire economy

**→ GAME USE:** Your central bank (the "Princesses") operates through a similar hidden mechanism. On the surface, they appear to be a passive institution managing currency. In reality, they have direct control over who gets money and who doesn't. This is their real power — not interest rates or speeches, but invisible quotas that determine which businesses thrive and which die.

### 2. Creating the Bubble (1980s)
- The BoJ deliberately inflated lending quotas, forcing banks to lend excessively
- This money was directed primarily into real estate and stocks
- The result: Tokyo land prices became absurd — one district was "worth" more than all of Canada, the Imperial Palace gardens worth more than California
- Stock market rose 240% between 1985-1989
- Economists at the time claimed this was due to "rising productivity" — they were blind to the real cause

**→ GAME USE:** Before your game begins, the central bank has been pumping money into specific sectors, creating apparent prosperity. NPCs talk about boom times. The economy looks healthy. But the player can find evidence of the artificial nature of this growth — lending quotas, pressure on banks.

### 3. Bursting the Bubble (1990s)
- The BoJ then restricted credit and burst the bubble
- Stock market dropped 80%, real estate fell 84%
- 212,000 companies went bankrupt
- The BoJ **could have fixed this immediately** — they had tools to inject money, buy assets, restart lending
- Instead, they deliberately sabotaged government recovery efforts
- When the Ministry of Finance tried fiscal stimulus, the BoJ quietly withdrew money on the other side
- They lowered interest rates (as ordered by the Ministry) but simultaneously reduced money in circulation

**→ GAME USE:** This is the key behavioral pattern for your Princesses. They appear to be trying to help — they make public announcements about recovery measures, lower rates, express concern. But behind the scenes, every recovery effort is quietly undermined. They drain money with one hand while publicly injecting it with the other. The Ministry/Government is trying to fix things and can't understand why nothing works.

### 4. Crisis as a Weapon — "Structural Reform"
- The BoJ's real goal was to force Japan to abandon its post-war economic model
- They wanted to replace the collaborative, government-guided system with American-style free market capitalism
- BoJ officials stated openly that reform wouldn't be accepted without crisis
- Toshihiko Fukui (who oversaw the disastrous lending during the bubble) later became Governor and stated he was "destroying the high-growth model" to "build a model that suits the new era"
- US pressure aligned with this — America had been demanding Japan open its economy for years
- The crisis made "structural reform" seem necessary and inevitable, when in fact the crisis itself was engineered

**→ GAME USE:** Your Princesses have an ideological agenda. They don't see themselves as villains — they believe the old system needs to be destroyed and replaced with something "better." They engineer suffering because they believe incremental reform is impossible. Only catastrophe can break existing power structures. This gives them genuine conviction, which makes them more interesting antagonists.

### 5. The "Princes" — An Inner Circle
- Werner identifies a select group of central bankers groomed from early in their careers
- Figures like Sasaki, Maekawa, Mieno, and Fukui
- They shared an ideology and a long-term plan
- They passed the agenda from one generation of leadership to the next
- They operated as a faction within the institution

**→ GAME USE:** Your Princesses aren't a monolithic organization. There's an inner circle — the "true believers" — who have been groomed and who share the real agenda. Most central bank employees have no idea what the inner circle is doing. This creates the possibility of allies within the bank who are as shocked as the player.

### 6. The Powerless Government
- The Ministry of Finance (the elected government's economic arm) was completely outmaneuvered
- They tried everything — fiscal packages, bailouts, stimulus
- Nothing worked because the central bank was secretly undermining every effort
- The Ministry didn't understand the real mechanism (credit creation) that controlled the economy
- The public blamed the government for the recession, not the central bank

**→ GAME USE:** The government in your game is genuinely trying to fix things but appears incompetent. The president and his ministers keep launching recovery plans that fail. The public loses faith. Meanwhile, the Princesses maintain their reputation as sober, responsible technocrats. The public doesn't blame them because they don't understand what the central bank actually does.

### 7. The Pattern Repeats
- Werner argues the same playbook was later used in the Asian financial crisis (1997)
- And then again in Europe — the ECB oversaw excessive credit growth in periphery countries (Ireland, Greece, Portugal, Spain) then withdrew it
- When the crisis hit, the ECB demanded sovereignty transfers and structural reforms as the price of assistance
- The pattern: inflate → crash → demand reforms during crisis → gain more power

**→ GAME USE:** If your world has other regions or nations, the central bank may have done this before. The player can find records of a previous "boom and bust" in another territory that followed the exact same pattern. This is the moment the conspiracy stops looking like coincidence and starts looking like a methodology.

# PART 3: SYNTHESIS — How These Two Stories Combine

## The Unified Plot Architecture

Both books describe the same fundamental dynamic: **an unaccountable institution destroys a leader/system using fabricated pretexts, while appearing to serve the public good.**

| Element | Nixon Conspiracy | Princes of the Yen | Your Game |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The target** | President Nixon | Japan's post-war economic system | Your president |
| **The conspirators** | Prosecutors, judges, congressional staff | Bank of Japan inner circle | The Princesses (central bank) + intelligence apparatus |
| **The weapon** | Fabricated legal case | Engineered economic crisis | Both — frame the president AND crash the economy |
| **The mechanism** | Secret Road Map, suppressed evidence | Window guidance, credit manipulation | Hidden financial orders + planted evidence |
| **The cover** | "We're upholding the law" | "We're managing the economy" | "We're protecting the people" |
| **The real goal** | Remove an inconvenient president | Force structural transformation | Reshape the political-economic order |
| **The public's role** | Believed the media narrative | Blamed the government, not the bank | The player starts by believing the official story |
| **The insider** | Shepard (noticed things didn't add up) | Werner (researched from inside the BoJ) | The whistleblower who contacts the player |

## The President's "Crime" — Combining Both Books

Here's how to make the frame-up feel real and layered:

1. **The central bank engineers an economic crisis** (Princes of the Yen playbook)
2. **The president tries to intervene** — maybe he tries to audit the central bank, or override their policies, or expose the window guidance equivalent
3. **This makes him the target** — the Princesses need him removed before he exposes them
4. **They fabricate a crime** (Nixon Conspiracy playbook) — perhaps they make it look like the president caused the economic crisis, or was corrupt, or committed some crime
5. **The investigation is pre-cooked** — the player is assigned to a case where the conclusion was written before the evidence was gathered
6. **The anonymous source guides the player** — feeding them "tips" that seem helpful but are actually steering toward the predetermined conclusion

## The Whistleblower's Role

Drawing from Shepard's own story: someone who was inside the system, initially believed in the mission, but started noticing inconsistencies. In your game:

- They were part of the conspiracy apparatus (maybe worked for the Princesses or the investigation)
- They started finding documents that contradicted the official case
- They can't go public (they'd be destroyed)
- So they leave cryptic messages for the player — someone they believe might eventually see the truth
- Their messages ("the future is in the past") aren't rebel slogans — they're specific clues pointing to evidence

## Central Bank Behavioral Design Checklist

To make the Princesses feel "Princes of the Yen authentic":

- [ ] They control credit through hidden, extralegal mechanisms, not just interest rates
- [ ] They have a public face (boring, technocratic, responsible) and a private agenda
- [ ] They genuinely believe they're doing the right thing — they see destruction as necessary medicine
- [ ] They have an inner circle groomed over generations
- [ ] Most of their employees are oblivious to the real agenda
- [ ] They can sabotage government economic policies without anyone understanding how
- [ ] They've done this before, in another context
- [ ] They speak in jargon that obscures what they actually do
- [ ] When things go wrong, the elected government gets blamed, not them
- [ ] Their ultimate goal is structural transformation — reshaping society according to their ideology

# PART 4: RECOMMENDED FURTHER READING

For the Watergate angle:
- **Silent Coup: The Removal of a President** by Len Colodny (1991) — CIA presence near Watergate, deeper intelligence angles
- **Without Honor** — Hillary Clinton's role on the investigation staff, political maneuvering
- **The Nixon Defense** by John Dean — Dean's own account, which ironically supports some of Shepard's arguments about the smoking gun tape being misinterpreted

For the central bank angle:
- **Princes of the Yen documentary** (2014, dir. Michael Oswald) — Available online, excellent visual companion to the book
- Werner's concept of **"window guidance"** is the single most important mechanism to understand for your game design

For story structure:
- The key insight from both sources: **the truth was hidden in plain sight for decades.** The evidence existed but nobody put it together because everyone accepted the official narrative. Your game should work the same way — on a second playthrough, the player should see that all the clues were there from the beginning.
