# Beatrice, round B1h (boots, knee pads, upper calf) — independent review

2026-10-08. I judged by eye from the pictures in `previews/`, with the B1g set taken from git (`81667fb`) for
before/after. I read no builder notes. I opened every picture named below whole, and cropped, gridded or
contrast-stretched the places in question (crops in `C:/tmp/beatrice_rev8/`: `z_*`, `g_*`, `ob_*`, `ba_*`).
References: `without_hair.png`, `without_hair_side.png`, `without_hair_back.png`. `concept.jfif` is cropped
above the knee and does not bear on this round. The low heel (about 3 cm) is the owner's ruling and is not
judged.

Measurements: `sheet.png`, `side.png` and `back.png` share the drawings' 1536-px frame, so 1 px is about
1.2 mm. I measured every row the same way for drawing, B1g and B1h: the outermost dark pixels of each leg
(the drawn outline included), checked against an overlay (`dbg_*.jpg`). This gives some drawing widths
different from the stage-7 table (for example the side calf is 113 px here, 122 there), so compare the columns
below with each other, not with stage 7. The side render stands about 19 px left of the side drawing; I
corrected for that wherever I compare positions.

## 2. The boots as a whole against the drawings

They now read as fitted patent ankle boots, not as cylinders slid over the leg. That is the main result of
the round. At `hero.jpg` and `legs.jpg` distance they are convincing: a clean patent surface, a curved instep,
an almond toe with a cap, a slim welt and a neat heel (`ob_hero.jpg`, `ob_side.jpg`, `ob_hero_back.jpg`).

Against the three drawings, what is still missing is the sculpture of the ankle:
- **The shaft top** is a level piping ring at the drawn height. It reads as a seam, not a rim. It is the
  brightest line below the knee in every full-body view, but it fits the design. It is fine as it is.
- **The shaft** is a constant tube from 1290 to 1370 px. The drawings show the ankle bones through the
  leather (front: both sides swell to 56 px at 1340; back: 55–62 px), and the reflections curve around them.
  In the render the reflections run dead straight, top to heel.
- **The heel cup** is smooth, round and seated (`boots_before_after.jpg` back, B1h). In the side view it
  flares out low behind a vertical shaft. The drawing's cup rises out of a groove at the Achilles; part of that
  difference is the drawing's high heel. Contrast-stretched, a faint vertical ridge shows on the outer side of
  the cup (`z_heelcup.jpg`). It may be a reflection edge. It is invisible in the plain pictures.
- **The vamp's reflections** are clean (see B1 above). **The toe** is right. **The welt** is right, apart from
  the small shelf at the back.
- **Not built, and not asked for:** the drawings cover the boot in piping: diamonds on the side
  (`without_hair_side.png` 1260–1420), stacked chevrons up the back, a V tongue under the top seam in front.
  The render's boot is plain apart from the toe cap. Whether the boot carries that pattern is a design
  question for the owner, not a defect.

## 3. The knee pads against the drawings

- **Width:** right (about 56 vs 49 px, within measurement error of a touch wide).
- **Dome:** present, but too much in the side view: about 18 mm proud of the leg, 7–10 mm ahead of the drawn
  knee line, with a 17 px step back onto the shin below it. The drawn pad follows the knee's own curve.
- **Finish:** the same black as the suit, while the drawing's pad is about a third lighter, with a bright
  centre highlight and a bright rim. This is the most visible difference left on the pad.
- **Frame below:** present and as drawn.

## 4. Above the knee: nothing moved

The B1g and B1h pictures are pixel-identical above the knee pads: `sheet.png`, `side.png` and `back.png`
differ by at most 6 grey levels in a handful of pixels above row 935/905/920. The first changed row in each
picture is the top of the pad. In `hero.jpg`, `legs.jpg` and `side.jpg` the only differences above the pads
are JPEG noise (at most 10 levels, mean signed difference 0.00), clustered next to the pads, where their
reflections and shadows changed. Between the calf change (ending at about 1140) and the boot top the lower leg
is also untouched. Nothing above the knee was broken.

## 5. Verdict

The boots are fixed in substance. They went from the most off-model object on her to a believable patent
boot, and the "thin shin" went with them. The knee pads are now the right size and shape from the front. The
lower leg is close to the pelvis's quality, but not quite there. The pelvis came out of its rounds with no
part reading as an add-on, while two things here still do: the knee pad stands out in the side view as a dark
shell on the knee, and the boot shaft is a perfect cylinder.

**What the owner will notice first:** the knee pads. In `hero.jpg` and `legs.jpg` they are the newest and most
prominent shapes on the leg, and they read as dark eggs rather than as the drawing's lighter, glossy shields.
In the side view they bulge. Second, in close-ups (`boot.jpg`, `boot_side.jpg`), the straight tube of the
shaft, with its straight reflection stripes.

## 6. Ranked corrections below the knee

1. **Knee pad: finish and side profile.** *Worth a short round (about 1 h, together with item 2).* Make the pad
   lighter than the suit, as drawn (drawing pad/suit luminance 1.33, render 1.0): a lighter or more reflective
   patent, a highlight down its centre and a brighter piped rim (`without_hair.png` 950–1040). Lower the dome
   so the pad sits about 5 mm proud, not about 18, and let its lower edge run into the shin, so the side
   outline is one convex knee line as in `without_hair_side.png` 900–1010. At present it is 7–10 mm ahead of
   that line, with a step of 17 px below (`g_knee_side_new.jpg`).
2. **Boot shaft: ankle anatomy.** *Worth the same round.* Add the ankle bones under the leather, about 3–4 mm
   a side at 1320–1350 px (front width 49 → about 55 px, `without_hair.png`), with the inner bone a little
   higher than the outer. Add a shallow groove up the back of the shaft above the heel cup, so the cup rises
   out of it as in `without_hair_side.png`/`_back.png`. The point is that the reflections should bend around
   the ankle instead of running straight from top to heel (`boot.jpg`, `boot_side.jpg`).
3. **Boot piping pattern** (the drawn diamonds, chevrons and V tongue). *A design question for the owner*:
   ask before building. If wanted, it is a round of its own.
4. **The front of the lower leg under the knee and the upper calf.** *Polish.* In front the render is 96 px
   just under the knee against 83 drawn (from before this round), and 104 against 97–99 at the upper calf
   (from this round). Narrowing the leg under the kneecap by a few px a side, at the frame's point, would give
   the drawn knee-then-calf rhythm. Do not take the calf back in the back or side views, where it now matches.
5. **Welt and heel cup details.** *Polish.* Pull the welt in to the heel cup at the back (now a 3–5 px shelf),
   and check the faint vertical ridge on the outer heel cup with the zebra view (`z_heelcup.jpg`; visible only
   contrast-stretched).
