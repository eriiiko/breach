2026-10-08. I judged by eye from the pictures in `previews/`. I read no builder notes. I opened every listed
picture whole, and cropped, contrast-stretched or high-passed the places in question (crops in
`C:/tmp/beatrice_rev7/`). References: `concept.jfif`, `good-body-silhouette.jfif`, the three `without_hair*`
drawings. Positions are given as places in the named picture.

Measurements: `sheet.png`, `side.png` and `back.png` are rendered into the same 1536-px frame as the three
drawings (the silhouettes line up to a few px), so pixel widths compare directly. In that frame the model
stands 1452 px tall (front) and her crotch is at 0.851 m (`lens.jpg`), so 1 px is about 1.2 mm. The
drawings' stature is inflated by their high heels, so I compare widths row by row, not as fractions of
stature, except where noted.

Five pictures carry a file date of 2026-10-04, not 10-07: `armpit_back.jpg`, `boot_side.jpg`,
`hand_palm.jpg`, `shoulder_back.jpg`, `top_close.jpg`. They may show an older build. I used them only where

## A2. Anything new this round broke

I found nothing that B1g clearly broke. I compared the b1f rows of `hips_before_after.jpg`, `waist.jpg`,
`bands_contrast.jpg` and `inner_thigh.jpg` with the B1g rows, plain and high-passed.

Three small things I saw; I cannot say they are new:
- **Terracing in the matte crotch hollow.** `lens.jpg` right panel, contrast-stretched: the soft hollow
  above the cusp shows fine horizontal ripples, about 2 mm apart, from about 0.86 to 0.90 m. A small chevron
  pinch sits at about 0.93 m on the centre line. The pinch is also in b1f (`hips_before_after.jpg` b1f front,
  high-passed). The ripples cannot be resolved in the b1f pictures that were kept. Neither shows without
  contrast stretch, and neither shows in gloss.
- **The two dark rounds under the seam V** (see N3). They read as the tops of the thighs reflecting dark,
  which is plausible. A slightly broader, flatter crotch under the V, as in the reference's U, would soften
  them.
- **A soft dark band remains across the belly in gloss.** It is the environment's horizon reflected on a
  surface that turns away. It has no edge any more, so it reads as shading, not as a belt (`hero.jpg`,
  `torso.jpg`).

## A3. Verdict

The waist is fixed. The belt line in gloss and the ring in matte are gone, and the torso now curves into the
hip as one long turn. The front hip is a little rounder just under the waist, but the outer thigh is still a
straight column for its upper half. The crotch details are cleaned to the point where they only show under
contrast stretch. The waist and pelvis are ready for the owner's go/no-go on the front and three-quarter
views. The side and seat are the open question that was left for him. His first remark will most likely be
about the side and back: next to the side drawing's round, high seat, hers is still small and flat, with no
under-curve. In front he will probably accept the waist. If anything in front catches him, it will be the
straight outer thigh.

# Part B — below the pelvis, and the arms and hands, for the next round

Ranked by how much each part spoils a hero or close-up shot, worst first.

## B1. Boots — the most "off-model" object on her

Pictures: `boot.jpg`, `boot_side.jpg` (dated 10-04, but it matches `side.jpg`), `legs.jpg`, `hero.jpg`, `side.png`.
The low heel (about 3 cm) is the owner's ruling and is not judged here.

- **The shaft is a straight tube that stands off the leg.** In `side.png` the shaft runs from about 1270 to
  1395 px as a constant-depth tube, 77 px front to back. The leg just above it is 66 px. So the tube stands
  about 13 mm proud, almost all of it behind the Achilles hollow. In front it is 52 px against 47 (about 6 mm
  in total). The drawings' widths are similar or larger (back view at the same height: 59 px drawn vs 52
  render). So it is **not too wide**. It is the wrong shape. In the drawings the shaft follows the ankle, hugs
  the Achilles, flares into a rounded heel cup, and runs down the front of the ankle into the instep in one
  curve (`without_hair_side.png` 1250–1400 px; my crop `c_feet_side_cmp.jpg`).
- **The top edge is a rim with a step**, with a V notch on one side (`boot.jpg` right boot, `boot_side.jpg`).
  In `legs.jpg` and `hero.jpg` the boots therefore read as separate cylinders slid over thin legs. The
  drawings put the boot top at the same height (front 1300 px, side about 1260 px). But there it is a seam
  ridge on a boot that continues the leg line.
- **The instep is a right angle.** In `side.png` the vertical shaft front meets the vamp in a corner at about
  1395 px. In the drawing it slopes down in a long curve.
- **The toe is a long, flat wedge.** From the instep corner to the tip is about 100 px, about 12 cm, thinning
  to a blade at the tip (`boot_side.jpg`). From the front it is broad and square (`boot.jpg` right). The
  drawings show an almond toe with some height to the toe box and a toe-cap seam. Foot length itself is fine:
  190 px (about 23 cm, 13% of stature), against 183 px in the drawing's foreshortened, heeled foot.
- **The vamp's surface is lumpy.** The reflections on the instep and toe break into blotches and dents
  (`boot.jpg` left boot at 2x, `boot_side.jpg` both toes). On a patent surface that reads as crumpled or
  melted.
- **The sole is a separate thin plate.** It sticks out as a shelf all round, with a dark gap line between it
  and the upper (`boot.jpg` left boot, at the waist of the sole), and it juts out behind over the heel block
  (`boot_side.jpg`). The drawings show a slim welt that follows the upper.
- **Good:** the shaft height, the heel height (as ruled), the overall foot length, the stance of the feet.

## B2. Hands — the second most visible fault, and the one most likely to be noticed in close-ups

Pictures: `hand.jpg`, `hand_palm.jpg` (dated 10-04), `hero.jpg`, `torso.jpg`, plus the matte hand in
`shape_vs_concept.jpg` side panels.

- **The surface is ring-banded.** In the matte side view (`shape_vs_concept.jpg` "default (B1g), side", at 3x)
  the back of the hand and every finger carry horizontal ridges, like stacked rings. In the glossy-lit
  `hand.jpg` the same shows as lumps along the thumb-side edge (around the middle of the left edge of the
  hand) and as ring creases on the fingers.
- **Size is right; the split between palm and fingers is not.** Wrist (cuff edge) to fingertip: render 133
  px, drawing 125 px in the same frame (`sheet.png` vs `without_hair.png`, left hand). That is +6%, about 16
  cm. Hand/forearm is 0.63 in the render and 0.62 in the drawing (forearm taken from the elbow fold to the
  cuff). So the hand is not too big or too small. (Hand/face is not a fair check: the placeholder head is
  about 18% shorter, chin to crown, than the drawing's.) But the fingers are 41% of the hand length in the
  render against 52% in the drawing (finger roots at about 775 px vs 745 px). The palm is a long box of
  constant width and the fingers are stubby.
- **The wrist is slightly thick**: 30 px vs 27 px drawn (+11%, about 4 mm). The palm hardly tapers from it.
- **No thumb reads.** In `sheet.png`, `hero.jpg` and `hand.jpg` the thumb is tucked behind the palm. The
  drawing's relaxed hand shows the thumb clearly, and the fingers are separated and curled at different
  depths (`without_hair.png`, both hands).
- **Pose:** the fingers hang clumped with the little finger splayed out, like a mannequin's
  (`hand_palm.jpg`, `hand.jpg`).
- **Cuff:** the size matches the drawing (33 vs 35 px). But in `hand.jpg` it is a hard double-ringed tube,
  with a dark gap between cuff and wrist on the little-finger side. Seat it snugly.
- **Good:** the hand's overall length and its placement at the thigh, the skin shading, the cuff height.

## B3. Collar and neck join

Pictures: `head.jpg`, `shoulder.jpg`, `torso.jpg`, `turnaround.png`; crop `c_collar_cmp.jpg`.

- The collar is a straight cylinder with a flat top lip and a ledge at its base. In `shoulder.jpg` the base
  casts a crisp shadow line on the chest, and the top shows an inner lip as a dark ring around the neck.
- The height is right: about 46 px (5.5 cm) in both the drawing and the render. The top width is 76 px vs
  70 px drawn, so the top stands off the neck by a few mm a side.
- In the drawings (`without_hair.png`, `good-body-silhouette.jfif`, the concept) the collar hugs the neck at
  the top and **flares into the trapezius and the shoulder line with no edge at its base**. Its base is well
  over 100 px wide.
- Where the shoulder panel's raglan seam reaches the collar on the viewer's right, the surface crumples
  (`shoulder.jpg`, right of the collar base).
- The neck shows between chin and collar top (about 14 px in `sheet.png`). In the drawing the collar meets the
  jaw. Leave that to the head stage, but note it so the collar is not cut down to suit a head that will
  change.
- **Good:** the collar height, the zip running up to the collar top with a flat pull.

## B4. Sleeve and shoulder surface: ripples in the gloss

Pictures: `flank.jpg`, `flank_back.jpg`, `shoulder.jpg`, `torso_back.jpg`.

- The highlight along the upper arm and deltoid breaks into fine horizontal bands (`flank.jpg` upper arm at 2x:
  a ladder of stripes down the highlight) and into a hammered, orange-peel mottle on the shoulder cap
  (`flank_back.jpg` left shoulder at 2x; `shoulder.jpg` right deltoid at 1.4x).
- On a glossy suit this is the most "CG" artefact left on the upper body. It is the same family as the hand's
  ring-banding: probably the same cause (ring-built tubes, too few rings, or a modifier stack).
- **Good:** the smooth forearms and the torso gloss.

## B5. Knee pads

Pictures: `knee.jpg`, `legs.jpg`, `sheet.png`; crop `c_knee_front_cmp.jpg`.

- **Position and height are right.** The pad is centred on the kneecap and at the drawn height (pad centre
  986 px vs 995 px drawn; the knee's centre line at about 410 px in both).
- **Too narrow.** The render's pad is about 40 × 88 px (0.43 × knee width). The drawing's is 52 × 90 px
  (0.54 × knee width). The render's oval is 1:2.2, the drawing's 1:1.7.
- **Too flat and too dark.** In front light (`sheet.png`) it nearly vanishes into the knee. The drawing's
  pad is a domed shield with a bright rim, framed by a larger piped outline that ends in a point on the shin.
- **Broken inner line.** At 2x, `knee.jpg` shows an inner oval drawn as a dashed, broken line on the pad's
  surface. It reads as a rendering error, not a seam.
- **Edge:** a thin step all round, acceptable. The diagonal strap piping from the pad to the outer thigh panel
  matches the drawing in spirit.
- **Good:** placement; the piping band around the knee.

## B6. Armpits, mesh inserts and piping elsewhere

Pictures: `torso_back.jpg`, `armpit_back.jpg` (10-04), `flank_back.jpg`, `torso.jpg`, `shoulder.jpg`. The
outer-thigh panel is not judged.

- **Back armpits:** where the shoulder-blade mesh insert ends at the arm, the seam is crumpled and jagged on
  both sides (`torso_back.jpg` at 1.6x, both armpits; worse in `armpit_back.jpg`). It reads as a tear.
- **Sawtooth piping:** at the upper tip of the shoulder-blade insert the piping zigzags (`flank_back.jpg`
  top left at 2x).
- **Doubled lines:** on the chest inserts, the insert's own piping and a separate seam run side by side with
  a gap between them at the upper end (`torso.jpg` viewer's right insert at 2x). It reads as two outlines.
- **Mesh material:** grey with coarse light dots, about 5 mm apart. It reads as a perforated metal sticker. In
  the drawings the mesh is a fine dark net with warm skin tone showing through (`without_hair.png` chest and
  waist inserts). Darker, finer and slightly warm would match.
- **Zips:** the front and back zips read as fine light stripes. They do not float, and the pulls lie flat.
  The drawings' zip is chunkier, but this is taste: leave it unless the owner asks.
- **Good:** the inserts' shapes and placement, the piping on the torso panels, the back zip.

## B7. Upper-arm seam and arm shape

Pictures: `torso.jpg`, `flank.jpg`, `shoulder.jpg`, `sheet.png` vs `without_hair.png`/`_back.png`
(crop `c_arm_cmp.jpg`).

- The render has a horizontal raised ring around the middle of the upper arm, at 425–438 px in `sheet.png`,
  with a V dip in front (`flank.jpg`). The drawings have no ring there. They have a diagonal seam on the upper
  arm (high at the outside, low at the armpit; clearest in `without_hair_back.png`) and an **elbow seam**
  with a soft fold (about 490 px front, 470 px back), which the render lacks.
- In gloss the ring is now a thin line, no longer the hard band of stage 6. It still reads as a garter.
- **Arm width:** the upper arm is 52 px vs 58 drawn at 440 px (−10%), the forearm 60 vs 65 at 520 px (−8%).
  The deltoid is less defined than in the drawing's rounded shoulder cap. That is mild and not worth a round
  of its own. If the sleeve is rebuilt for B4, add about 5% to the upper arm and a little deltoid then.
- The cuffs sit about 20 px (2.4 cm) lower than drawn and the fingertips likewise. The arms hang a little
  steeper or are a little longer. That is mild; check it when the hands are rebuilt.

## B11. Recommended build rounds

1. **Legs: boots + knee pads (+ calf +5%)**, about 2.5–3 h.
   - Boots: refit the shaft to the ankle and Achilles, seat its top edge into the suit as a seam ridge (no
     rim), curve the instep, make an almond toe with a toe box, merge sole and upper into a slim welt, and give
     the vamp a clean surface.
   - Knee pads: widen to about 0.55 × knee width, give them a dome, remove the dashed inner line, and add the
     drawn outer frame if it is cheap.
   - Why first: the boots are the most off-model object in every full-body picture, and they cause the "thin
     shin" impression. Everything here is below the knee, independent of the pelvis the owner is judging, and
     checkable on `boot*.jpg`, `legs.jpg`, `knee.jpg` and the side/back sheets.
2. **Hands + cuff**, about 2–3 h.
   - Remove the ring-banding.
   - Lengthen the fingers to about half the hand length, keeping the overall length.
   - Taper the palm, slim the wrist by about 3 mm, and bring the thumb out.
   - Pose the hand relaxed, as in `without_hair.png`.
   - Seat the cuff with no gap.
   - Why second: this is topology work of its own and the second most visible fault. Skin draws the eye in
     every torso and close-up shot. If the hand's banding and the sleeve's ripples turn out to have one cause,
     fix the cause here and carry it into round 3.
3. **Upper body surface: collar + sleeves + armpits + piping**, about 2–3 h.
   - Collar: flare the base into the trapezius with no ledge and hug the neck at the top.
   - Remove the gloss ripples on the upper arm and deltoid.
   - Replace the mid-arm ring with the drawn diagonal upper-arm seam plus an elbow seam.
   - Clean the back-armpit tears, the sawtooth piping tip and the doubled chest-insert lines.
   - Optionally, a darker, finer, warmer mesh.
   - Why last: each item is smaller and they share one area and one set of pictures (`shoulder*.jpg`,
     `torso*.jpg`, `flank*.jpg`, `head.jpg`). The collar is best finished before the head stage, which will
     sit on it.

Keep the hips and the bust out of all three rounds until the owner has ruled.
