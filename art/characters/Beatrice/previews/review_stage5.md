# Beatrice, round B1e (the leg gap, side profile, front flare) — independent review

2026-10-06. Judged by eye only, from the pictures in `previews/` (all listed ones opened whole, the
pelvis ones also cropped 2–3x and contrast-stretched) against `concept.jfif`,
`good-body-silhouette.jfif` and the three `without_hair*` drawings. No builder notes read.
Picture coordinates below are given as positions in the named picture (e.g. "hips.jpg, front
panel, at the leg gap").

## 1. Verdict

Not yet ready to show as "the hips round". Front and back are clean: no corner anywhere, the cleft
is soft, the size is right. But the leg gap, the thing he asked for first, has turned from a
pointed V into a narrow, parallel-sided slot with a round end, like a slot cut between the legs,
with a dark centre crease above it that shows as a ridge in the gloss. The side profile is
practically unchanged from stage 4. His first remark would most likely be about the gap ("that
looks like a slot, her thighs should just meet"), and his second that from the side she is still a
tube.

## 3. New defects, worst first

**A1. The leg gap is a slot, not touching thighs** — SHAPE.
`crotch.jpg` front matte and back matte; `hips.jpg` front and back panels at the gap.
The model: a parallel-walled slit about 1–1.5 cm wide and about 3 cm long, ending in a tight
semicircle (it looks like a slot cut with a round-ended tool). At its bottom both walls bend outward
in a *concave* curve (a bell or trumpet shape) and join the straight V of stage 4. The slot looks the
same from front and back. The inner thigh outline therefore goes *concave* just below the crotch,
which is where the adductor mass should make it *convex*.
The references: in `good-body-silhouette.jfif` and the concept the thighs meet: no light shows
through for the first few centimetres below the crotch, and the crotch reads as a broad soft U of
the suit, not an opening. In `without_hair.png`, too, the inner thighs touch below the crotch and
then part as a slim lens whose sides bulge toward each other. Note that the B1e slot follows the
modelling drawing's orange outline closely (`shape_vs_concept.jpg` front), and that outline is the
stiff part of the drawing: the concept and the silhouette show no slot.

**A2. Centre crease above the gap (front)** — SURFACE.
`hips.jpg` front panel just above the gap; `crotch.jpg` front matte and "from below, front";
`crotch.jpg` front glossy at the point where the two seams meet.
Matte: a dark vertical crease runs up 2–3 cm from the top of the slot into a darker hollow, which is
deeper than in stage 4 (compare the top and bottom rows of `hips_before_after.jpg` front). From below
it reads as a split between two rounded lobes. Glossy: a thin vertical highlight ridge runs up through
the seam V with a small arrow-shaped highlight at its top, and a bright cap sits on the slot's round end.
This is a pinch in the surface, not a soft form. The references show a smooth crotch panel; the
concept at most a broad, soft suggestion.

**A3. Seat and lower back read as one dome in the back views** — SHAPE.
`hero_back.jpg` pelvis; `torso_back.jpg`; `back_vs_reference.png`.
In the gloss one broad highlight band runs from the lower back over the seat. The two buttocks hardly
separate except for the short cleft, and the seat has no lower edge of its own (see the fold above).
The back drawing, wide as it is, shows two round lobes with a crisp under-curve, and that *shape*
(not the size) is what is missing. This was implied in stage 4's fold item, but it is more visible now
that the cleft is soft.

**A4. Back zip pull stands straight out of the lower back** — DETAIL.
`side.jpg` at waist height behind the arm; `shape_vs_concept.jpg` and `hips_vs_drawings.jpg` side
panels; `side_vs_reference.png`.
A thin rectangular tab sticks out horizontally about 3–4 cm from the back at the waist, like a pin.
It is the lower of the two small tabs seen on the back seam in `torso_back.jpg`. A zip pull lies flat
against the suit.

## 4. How far the front curve should go

**Further: take the early flare the rest of the way to the frontal reference, in the band between the
narrowest waist and the top of the hip only, and keep the widest width as it is.**
Reason, from `shape_vs_silhouette.jpg`: in the reference the side line turns outward right under
its narrowest point and is already convex by navel height, so waist and hip form one S. In B1e the
line below the narrowest waist (1.14 m) still runs almost vertical for roughly the next 8–10 cm,
then rises to full width over a short distance near crotch height. That straight run plus the late
rise is what still reads as a cone. The half step taken this round shows no corner and does not
make her look wider (`hips_before_after.jpg` front), so there is visible room for the other half. The
concept's near hip (`concept.jfif`, viewer's right) confirms a high, round hip. Do not push the
widest point outward, and do not add width below the crotch.

## 5. Good, do not change

- Front and back outlines: no corner at crotch height or anywhere else from waist to knee, matte
  or glossy (`hips.jpg` front/back, `hips_before_after.jpg`).
- The back view's single convex line from waist over hip into thigh (`hips.jpg` back).
- The overall hip width and size: the "one step slimmer" build reads right.
- The cleft: short and soft (`hips.jpg` back and three-quarter back).
- The side outline under the seat: the notch is gone; keep that run smooth while the rest of the side
  changes.
- The round top of the gap. Whatever replaces the slot must not bring back a point.
- The glossy seat and lower belly: unbroken, smooth highlights, no bands in gloss (`hero_back.jpg`,
  `crotch.jpg` glossy).

## 6. Corrections for one more round, in order

1. **Leg gap: let the thighs meet.** Replace the slot and its concave flare with inner thighs that
   touch, with no light through them, for a few centimetres below the crotch from front and back.
   Then they part as a slim lens whose sides bulge toward each other (convex inner thighs), widening
   smoothly toward the knee. In the same pass remove the centre crease above it: the matte dark line
   and the glossy ridge with its arrow-shaped tip at the seam V (A1, A2).
2. **Side profile: the outline barely moved this round.** Give the front of the thigh a convex swell
   from the groin crease to above the knee. Shape the seat as a fuller round that rises out of the
   lumbar hollow (lumbar a little deeper) and tucks under into the back of the thigh with a soft
   under-curve. The under-curve must not come back as a notch. It is also what gives the back and
   three-quarter back views the seat's lower edge (the fold item, and A3). Keep the hip width; take the
   extra seat depth only part of the way toward the side drawing, which is known too full.
3. **Front: finish the flare** between the narrowest waist and the top of the hip as in section 4,
   widest width unchanged, and let the outer thigh taper slightly from hip to knee instead of the
   parallel column. Clear the horizontal shading rings on the thigh in three-quarter front in the
   same pass.
