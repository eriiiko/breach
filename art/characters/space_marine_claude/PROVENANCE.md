## Reproduce

Blender 4.5.10 LTS (`C:/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 4.5/blender.exe`), bundled Python.

    cd art/characters/space_marine_claude
    ./run.sh --sheet --beauty all --samples 128 --save      # everything, about 2 minutes on an RTX 3070
    ./run.sh --draft --sheet                                  # 9-second iteration loop

`run.sh` wraps `blender -b --factory-startup -P scripts/build.py -- <options>`; `build.py --help`
text is in its docstring. `scripts/ref_measure.py` prints the artwork's silhouette widths per height.

Outputs: `previews/` (tracked) and `source/space_marine.blend` (27 MB, gitignored — it is
regenerated by `--save`) plus `source/mesh_stats.json` (tracked).

## How it is built

- `../charkit/kit.py` — the modelling kit, shared with the other characters. A `Loft` is a tube of superellipse sections along a
  centre line, kept as an analytic surface `(phi, t) -> point, normal`. Armour plates, straps,
  pockets and pouches are built in a metric frame anchored ON those surfaces (`OnLoft`,
  `OnEllipsoid`, `OnPlane`), so they follow the body they sit on. Cloth folds are authored
  ridges displaced along the loft normal; `flex` and `seam` are per-vertex attributes the
  materials read (black ribbed stretch panels, stitched seam lines).
- `scripts/marine.py` — the character: every dimension in metres, fitted to the artwork.
  Limbs are modelled on the left and mirrored. The trousers are one welded surface with a
  real centre seam (each leg's section grows into half the pelvis and is clamped at the mid-plane).
- `scripts/scene.py` — the marine's materials, the orthographic re-shoot of the sheet and the
  silhouette comparison; studio rig, cameras and material builders are `../charkit/studio.py`.
- `scripts/refkit.py` — the reference sheet's pixel frame and its figure mask.

## Measured

- Silhouette intersection-over-union against the artwork, same framing: front 0.923, side 0.937,
  back 0.922. The remainder is mostly the artwork's floor shadow (counted as figure by the mask)
  and the backpack, whose top the artwork draws at 1.71 m in the side view and about 1.62 m in
  the back view; the model takes 1.675 m.
- Mesh (`source/mesh_stats.json`): 106 mesh objects, 685,976 source triangles, 1,637,804 after
  modifiers. Suit 302 k, boots 140 k, armour 96 k, helmet 88 k, gloves 31 k, pack 29 k (source).
- The saved `.blend` was reopened in a fresh Blender: 112 objects, 106 meshes, 9 materials,
  no armature, no emissive material.

## Not done, and known weaknesses

- No UV maps, no texture bake, no rig, no skin weights, no glTF export, no engine test. The
  Quaternius 53-bone skeleton (`assets/models/marine/`) is untouched and nothing is bound to it.
- This is a high-resolution source, not a game mesh: a 4 mm grid on the suit. A game asset needs
  retopology or decimation and a normal-map bake of the folds and plate edges.
- Parts overlap rather than weld (except the trousers); loft ends are open where a neighbour
  hides them. Fine for rendering, not for deformation.
- The fine wrinkling is half geometry (authored folds), half shader bump; the bump does not export.
- Materials are Cycles node graphs (ambient-occlusion and bevel nodes): they do not carry to
  the game's `marine_shader.py`.
- Against the artwork: the collar mantle and knee pads are rounder than the artwork's faceted
  plates; the boots have fewer layered panels; the cloth is less heavily crumpled; the shin's
  side zip and the small helmet vents are missing. The model is exactly left-right symmetric.
